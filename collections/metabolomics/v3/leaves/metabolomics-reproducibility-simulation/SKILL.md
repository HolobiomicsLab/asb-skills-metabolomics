# metabolomics-reproducibility-simulation

## Summary

Reproduces Simulation I of the MaRR metabolomics reproducibility paper: simulates 1000 datasets of M=2860 metabolites from bivariate Normal reproducible/irreproducible mixture settings mimicking BioTech-like data, applies the nonparametric MaRR maximal rank statistic, and evaluates FDR control, discriminative power, and pi_1 bias.

## When to use

Use when validating that the MaRR procedure controls FDR at a nominal level, achieves high discriminative power, and estimates the reproducible-signal proportion (pi_1) without substantial bias under simulated bivariate Normal metabolomics settings.

## When NOT to use

- The goal is assessing reproducibility on real MS-Metabolomics datasets (Tech, BioTech, Bio) rather than validating the procedure on simulated data with known pi_1 ground truth.
- The data are not generated from the bivariate Normal reproducible/irreproducible mixture design, so bias (estimated minus true reproducible proportion) cannot be computed.
- A comprehensive evaluation of pre-processing steps (imputation, normalization) is the goal; the paper states this was not the purpose of the simulations.

## Inputs

- Simulated bivariate Normal metabolomics datasets (M=2860 metabolites) with reproducible component (mu_R in {3.89, 4.01, 4.13}, sigma_R=0.17, rho_R in {0.45, 0.99}) and irreproducible component (mu_IR=3.2, sigma_IR=0.05)
- pi_1 proportion of reproducible metabolites in {0.2, 0.4, 0.75, 0.9}
- R scripts SIM_B1.R ... SIM_C1.R ... SIM_D4.R and source_MaRR.R from the Simulations folder of Ghoshlab/marr_paper_evaluations

## Outputs

- .Rdata result objects per setting (e.g., B1_sim_results.Rdata, C1_sim_results.Rdata, D4_sim_results.Rdata)
- Per-setting empirical FDR at the 0.05 level, discriminative power (1-NDR), and bias of the estimated reproducible proportion over 1000 replicates
- Simulation plots generated by SIMPLOT_A45.R, SIMPLOT_A99.R and SIMPLOT_B.R for the 3 simulation designs

## How to apply

Source the MaRR functions from 'source_MaRR.R' in the repository's 'src' folder. For each of the 24 Simulation I settings — mu_R in {3.89, 4.01, 4.13} x rho_R in {0.45, 0.99} x pi_1 in {0.2, 0.4, 0.75, 0.9}, with mu_IR=3.2, sigma_R=0.17, sigma_IR=0.05 held fixed — simulate 1000 datasets of M=2860 metabolites split into reproducible and irreproducible components according to pi_1. Apply the nonparametric MaRR maximal rank statistic to each simulated dataset to estimate the reproducibility signal proportion. At the nominal 0.05 significance level, compute empirical FDR, discriminative power (proportion of true reproducible features detected), and bias (estimated minus true reproducible proportion) over the 1000 replicates per setting. Save aggregated per-setting results as .Rdata objects (e.g., B1_sim_results.Rdata, C1_sim_results.Rdata, D4_sim_results.Rdata) using the SIM_*.R scripts, then generate plots with the SIMPLOT_A45.R, SIMPLOT_A99.R and SIMPLOT_B.R scripts. The rationale is that Simulation I mimics MS-Metabolomics data with gradual reduction in correlation between replicate pairs for less highly ranked signals, stress-testing MaRR's FDR calibration in realistic conditions.

## Related tools

- **marr** (Provides the nonparametric maximal rank statistic used to estimate the reproducibility signal proportion in each simulated dataset and control FDR at the 0.05 level) — http://bioconductor.org/packages/marr
- **R** (Runtime environment (>=4.0) for running the simulation scripts and aggregating FDR, power, and bias results)

## Examples

```
source('src/source_MaRR.R'); Rscript SIM_B1.R  # generates B1_sim_results.Rdata for one Simulation I setting
```

## Evaluation signals

- Across all 24 Simulation I settings the empirical FDR is at or below the nominal 0.05 level
- Discriminative power (1-NDR) is near 100%, except at the smallest pi_1 values where power drops
- Bias of the estimated reproducible signal proportion lies within the narrow range (-0.05, 0.05) for every setting
- Each setting is based on 1000 simulated replicate datasets of M=2860 metabolites split into reproducible and irreproducible components by pi_1
- Result objects are produced by the SIM_*.R scripts and plots by the three SIMPLOT scripts, matching the paper's figure organization

## Limitations

- True signals may fall in undetectable regions due to overlap (lack of separation) between the reproducible and irreproducible components
- High power is not guaranteed at small values of the proportion of reproducible metabolites (small pi_1)
- The simulation evaluates specific parametric settings (BioTech-like bivariate Normal); performance under other dependence structures is not established here
