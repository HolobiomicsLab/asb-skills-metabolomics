# BioTransformer one-step transformation recapitulation

## Summary

Validates whether molecules generated by a chemical language model (e.g. DeepMet/CLM) correspond to known metabolic transformation products by comparing them against one-step BioTransformer-predicted transformation products of known metabolites, reporting the fraction recapitulated (target ≈77.5%).

## When to use

Use when you have a set of generated SMILES and a set of known metabolite SMILES and need to verify that the generated structures resemble plausible metabolic transformation products rather than arbitrary synthetic compounds.

## When NOT to use

- Input molecules are experimentally observed MS/MS peaks or spectra rather than a pool of generated SMILES — use spectral library matching instead.
- You need to predict transformations for a specific metabolite, not assess the global biosynthetic plausibility of a generator; run BioTransformer directly on the molecule of interest.
- The model was trained on a metabolome very distant from human (e.g. plant or bacterial), where the one-step transformation rules calibrated on human metabolism may not transfer.

## Inputs

- Generated SMILES strings sampled from a trained chemical language model (e.g. 500,000 samples from a DeepMet/CLM RNN checkpoint)
- Known metabolite SMILES from the withheld test fold (e.g. HMDB structures)
- BioTransformer-predicted one-step transformation products of those known metabolites

## Outputs

- Recapitulation metric (percentage of generated molecules that are one-step BioTransformer transformation products of known metabolites, expected ~77.5%)
- metabolite_likeness_evaluation.csv row containing the recapitulation value alongside random-forest AUC and nearest-neighbour Tanimoto similarity

## How to apply

Sample SMILES from the trained CLM model checkpoint (e.g. 500,000 SMILES per fold via the clm sampling routine), canonicalize them with RDKit, and remove any structure whose InChIKey matches the training set for that fold. For each remaining generated molecule, query BioTransformer (or the precomputed transformation product database) to determine whether it corresponds to a one-step transformation product of a known metabolite from the withheld fold. Recapitulation is expressed as the percentage of sampled molecules that are BioTransformer one-step transformation products of known metabolites. The article reports approximately 77.5% recapitulation, which serves as the expected benchmark for a model that has learned biosynthetic logic; values substantially lower suggest the generator is producing chemically valid but biosynthetically implausible structures.

## Examples

```
snakemake --cores 1 clm/results/evaluate --config fold=1 train_seed=0
```

## Evaluation signals

- Recapitulation fraction reaches approximately 77.5% of sampled molecules being one-step BioTransformer transformation products of known metabolites
- All generated molecules are canonicalized by RDKit and none of their InChIKeys appear in the training set for the fold being evaluated
- The recapitulation value is written alongside the random-forest AUC (~0.48) and nearest-neighbour Tanimoto similarity in metabolite_likeness_evaluation.csv

## Limitations

- The language model was trained and evaluated on human metabolites; recapitulation thresholds calibrated here may not transfer to evolutionarily distant metabolomes (plant or bacterial).
- Inherent limitations exist for the model's ability to anticipate metabolites that originate from divergent, as-of-yet undiscovered biosynthetic routes, which BioTransformer would also not predict.
- Only one-step transformation products are tested, so multi-step metabolic pathways are out of scope.
