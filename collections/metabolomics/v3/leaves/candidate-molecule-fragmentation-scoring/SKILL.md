# candidate-molecule-fragmentation-scoring

## Summary

Ranks candidate molecules by matching MS/MS (and MSn) peak m/z values against in silico generated substructures, converting match quality into an intensity-weighted penalty score per candidate. This skill underpins structure annotation in MAGMa when no reference spectra exist.

## When to use

Use when you have MS/MS or MSn peak lists (hierarchical m/z:intensity parenthesis format) for an unknown precursor, a set of candidate structures from PubChem/cheminformatics database, and you need to rank candidates by how well the observed fragment peaks are explained by in silico generated substructures.

## When NOT to use

- When reference spectra of known standards are available and can be matched directly — MAGMa is for the case where spectra must be annotated solely from candidate structures without informative reference spectra
- When the precursor formula is unknown and no candidate set can be assembled from an accurate-mass database search — scoring cannot proceed without candidates
- When peaks arise from fragmentation pathways requiring more than 3 bond dissociations plus hydroxyl/primary amine neutral losses (e.g. challenge 16's higher-energy peaks at m/z 122.0408, 178.03072, etc. remained unexplained under default settings)

## Inputs

- MS/MS or MSn peak lists (converted to MAGMa hierarchical m/z:intensity parenthesis input format)
- Candidate molecule structures (SDF format from PubChem, or local PubChem snapshot; compounds with elements C,H,N,O,P,S, up to 1200 Da)
- Precursor molecular formula / accurate mass to restrict candidate retrieval
- Number of bond dissociations to allow (default up to 3)
- Mass tolerance (ppm and absolute minimum, e.g. 10 ppm / 0.002 Da, or 0.005 Da for challenge 11)

## Outputs

- Ranked list of candidate molecules ordered by reversed intensity-weighted penalty score
- Penalty score per candidate (intensity-weighted average of substructure penalties, +10 per unmatched peak)
- Best-scoring substructure annotation for each MS/MS peak
- Ties broken by PubChem refscore

## How to apply

For each candidate structure retrieved (e.g. from a local PubChem database snapshot of 2 December 2013), MAGMa fragments the molecule systematically — substructures generated by applying up to 3 bond dissociations, then expanded with single losses of hydroxyl groups (representing neutral loss of water) or primary amine groups (representing neutral loss of ammonia) — so each substructure carries a penalty summing the penalty values for all dissociated bonds. Observed MS/MS peak m/z values are matched with the best scoring substructures, enforcing an allowed mass deviation of 10 ppm with an absolute minimum of 0.002 Da (or a minimum of 0.005 Da when unexplained peaks suggest a too-restrictive tolerance, as in challenge 11). Each peak's match penalty is intensity weighted and averaged to yield a total penalty score per candidate; a penalty of 10 is assigned to non-matched peaks. Candidates are then ranked by reversed penalty score, with equal-scoring candidates ordered by their PubChem refscore; this ranking is what was submitted to the CASMI 2013 contest. When peaks remain unexplained, do not discard the correct structure — instead revisit the mass tolerance and the degree of in silico fragmentation before concluding a candidate should be absent.

## Examples

```
magmaweb --peaklist challenge11.msms --candidates candidates.sdf --mass-tolerance 10ppm --min-tolerance 0.002
```

## Evaluation signals

- The correct candidate should receive the lowest (best) penalty score; in 6 of the 12 submitted CASMI 2013 category 2 solutions it was ranked 1st
- Every MS/MS peak above the mass deviation threshold should be matched to a substructure or explicitly flagged as non-matched (penalty 10)
- Equal penalty scores across candidates must be resolved by PubChem refscore ordering, not left arbitrary
- For challenge 11, re-running with a minimum tolerance of 0.005 Da (instead of 0.002 Da) should rank the two demethoxycurcumin tautomers 1st and 2nd
- Matched fragment m/z values must fall within the stated mass deviation (e.g. 10 ppm with a 0.002 Da absolute minimum) of the theoretical substructure m/z

## Limitations

- Default degree of in silico fragmentation (up to 3 bond dissociations plus hydroxyl/amine neutral losses) was insufficient for some challenges — challenge 16's higher-energy peaks could not be explained under these settings
- Mass tolerance too restrictive (10 ppm / 0.002 Da) can cause unexplained peaks and poor ranking even when the correct candidate is present in the candidate set
- Successful annotation required inclusion of the relevant candidate molecules, appropriate mass tolerance, and sufficient in silico fragmentation; missing any of these three fails
- Performance on MS/MS data was demonstrated to be comparable to other methods, but the algorithm was originally developed for MSn multistage fragmentation data with n>2
