# Synthetic spectrum dataset partitioning

## Summary

Partitions large synthetic GC-EI-MS training corpora (ZINC-derived synth1 and synth2 spectra generated by NEIMS and RASSP) into 0.9:0.05:0.05 train/validation/test splits with compound-matched partitions across both spectrum generators, enabling leakage-free evaluation of SpecTUS-style models.

## When to use

Use when preparing large synthetic EI-MS corpora (multiple spectrum generators over the same compounds) for model pretraining or held-out evaluation and compound-level leakage between generators must be controlled.

## When NOT to use

- Partitioning experimental spectra (e.g., the NIST 20 finetuning set), which uses a different 0.8:0.1:0.1 ratio with duplicates forced into the same split
- The input is a single-generator spectrum collection with no paired generator halves — the compound-matched cross-generator constraint is then moot
- You already downloaded the released synth1/synth2 splits from the Hugging Face Hub (MS-ML); re-partitioning them would break comparability with published metrics

## Inputs

- ZINC20-derived compound lists synth1 (~4.7M compounds) and synth2 (~4.8M compounds) as SMILES sets
- NEIMS-generated synthetic spectra for each compound in synth1/synth2
- RASSP-generated synthetic spectra for each compound in synth1/synth2
- NIST 20 compound structures for overlap/leakage exclusion

## Outputs

- 0.9:0.05:0.05 train/val/test compound-level partitions of synth1 and synth2, shared across NEIMS and RASSP halves
- Held-out synthetic test partition (5% of each synthetic dataset) formatted for downstream Acc1/Acc10 evaluation
- Leakage-filtered corpus (no NIST 20 compounds, no deuterium-containing compounds, within m/z 500 / 300-peak / 100-char SMILES limits) publishable on the Hugging Face Hub

## How to apply

Start from the synthetic compound sets synth1 (~4.7M compounds) and synth2 (~4.8M compounds), obtained by filtering ~30M scraped ZINC20 SMILES down to compounds RASSP can process, and generate spectra for every compound with both custom-trained NEIMS and RASSP forward models. Partition each synthetic dataset by compound with a 0.9:0.05:0.05 train/validation/test ratio, and — critically — apply the SAME compound split to the NEIMS-generated and RASSP-generated halves so that the two generator halves share identical compound assignments (each held-out test partition is 5% of each dataset). Before splitting, purge any compounds overlapping evaluation/reference data: remove all NIST 20 compounds to prevent data leakage, drop compounds containing deuterium, and enforce model-specific limits (max m/z 500, max 300 peaks, max SMILES length 100 characters). Split at the compound level rather than the spectrum level, because each compound contributes paired spectra from two generators; splitting independently per generator would leak ground-truth structures between pretraining and the held-out synthetic test split used for Acc1/Acc10 evaluation.

## Related tools

- **NEIMS** (Forward model producing one of the two paired synthetic spectra per compound for the corpus being partitioned)
- **RASSP** (Forward model producing the second paired synthetic spectra per compound; also defines the compound-admissibility filter (8 most common elements, possible subformulae))
- **SpecTUS** (Downstream transformer whose pretraining/evaluation splits these partitions define; split consistency determines reported Acc1/Acc10 validity) — https://github.com/hejjack/SpecTUS
- **Hugging Face Hub (MS-ML)** (Publication venue for the 17.2M synthetic spectra and pretrained checkpoint using these partitions) — https://huggingface.co/MS-ML

## Evaluation signals

- Partition sizes match a 0.9:0.05:0.05 train/val/test ratio for both synth1 (~4.7M compounds) and synth2 (~4.8M compounds)
- Compound identity sets of the test partitions are identical between the NEIMS-generated and RASSP-generated halves of each synthetic dataset
- Zero compound overlap between any pretraining partition and NIST 20 compounds, and no deuterium-containing compounds anywhere in the corpus
- All retained spectra respect max m/z 500, max 300 peaks, and max SMILES length 100 characters
- Reproduced Acc1 on the held-out synthetic test split is consistent with published end-of-pretraining exact-reconstruction rates (38% RASSP-generated, 29% NEIMS-generated)

## Limitations

- The RASSP-processability filter restricts the synthetic corpora to compounds of the 8 most common elements, so partitioning inherits this chemical coverage bias
- Spectra-quality sensitivity of downstream models means test partitions drawn from less-curated sources (e.g., Cayman, MONA-style data) perform poorly compared with rigorously curated libraries
- The synthetic test split measures only synthetic-forward-model performance; NIST spectra reconstruction at end of pretraining is far lower (3%)
