# false-discovery-rate-empirical-calibration

## Summary

Empirically calibrates the False Discovery Rate of the nonparametric MaRR maximal rank statistic by simulating replicate-pair data whose correlation decays with rank, then aggregating empirical FDR, power (1-NDR), and bias across settings.

## When to use

Use when assessing whether a reproducibility-detection procedure controls FDR at the nominal level under rank-dependent correlation decay between replicate pairs, e.g., pi_1 x r_0 simulation grids.

## When NOT to use

- The goal is a comprehensive evaluation of metabolomics pre-processing steps (BPCA/kNN imputation, quantile/median/RUV normalization) — the paper states its goal was only to benchmark comparisons, not to comprehensively evaluate pre-processing.
- Reproducible and irreproducible components strongly overlap (lack of separation), which the authors note can push true signals into undetectable regions and distort calibration.
- The true proportion of reproducible signals (pi_1) is very small — MaRR power drops in these settings, so FDR/power curves alone should not be read as a calibration failure.

## Inputs

- Simulated high-dimensional metabolomics count matrices with replicate-pair correlation decreasing linearly with rank (pi_1 in {0.2,0.4,0.75,0.9} x r_0 in {0.4,0.6,0.99}, 1000 replicates per setting)
- Sourced MaRR maximal rank statistic functions (source_MaRR.R from the 'src' folder)
- Nominal FDR level alpha (default 0.05 via Marr()/MaRR procedure)

## Outputs

- Per-setting R result objects 'B1_sim_results.Rdata' ... 'D4_sim_results.Rdata' containing empirical FDR, power (1-NDR), and bias
- FDR, power, and bias plots for Simulation II generated by SIMPLOT_B.R (comparable to Figs. 3-4 and S6-S7)

## How to apply

Source the MaRR maximal-rank functions from 'source_MaRR.R' in the repository 'src' folder. For each of the 12 Simulation II settings crossing pi_1 in {0.2, 0.4, 0.75, 0.9} with r_0 in {0.4, 0.6, 0.99} — where replicate-pair correlation decreases linearly with rank down to r_0 — run the corresponding SIM_B1.R ... SIM_D4.R script to generate 1000 simulated datasets, applying MaRR to each to estimate the proportion of reproducible signals and detect reproducible features. Save each setting's aggregated empirical FDR, power (1-NDR), and bias (estimated minus true reproducible proportion) as the matching R object (B1_sim_results.Rdata ... D4_sim_results.Rdata). Run the SIMPLOT_B.R script on the saved objects to generate FDR, power, and bias plots. The calibration is judged correct when empirical FDR stays at or below the nominal alpha (default 0.05), typically behaving conservatively when pi_1 is high and r_0 is low, since the nonparametric maximal rank statistic makes no parametric assumptions on distributions or dependence structures.

## Related tools

- **marr** (Provides the nonparametric maximal rank reproducibility statistic whose FDR, power, and bias are being calibrated; installed from Bioconductor or GitHub) — http://bioconductor.org/packages/marr
- **R** (Runtime environment (>=4.0 required) for running the SIM_B1.R-SIM_D4.R simulation scripts and aggregating FDR/power/bias into .Rdata objects)
- **marr_paper_evaluations** (Repository containing the simulation scripts (SIM_B1.R...SIM_D4.R, SIMPLOT_B.R) and 'src/source_MaRR.R' used to execute the FDR calibration grid) — github.com/Ghoshlab/marr_paper_evaluations

## Examples

```
Rscript -e "source('src/source_MaRR.R'); source('SIM_B1.R'); source('Output/SIMPLOT_B.R')"
```

## Evaluation signals

- Empirical FDR is at or below the nominal level across all 12 Simulation II settings (pi_1 x r_0 grid)
- FDR shows conservative behavior when pi_1 is high and r_0 is low
- Power (1-NDR) is high in most settings except at the smallest pi_1 values
- Bias (estimated minus true proportion of reproducible signals) is close to zero with the characteristic negative-bias pattern at low pi_1
- Reproduced plots match Figs. 3-4 and S6-S7 from the paper

## Limitations

- True signals may fall in undetectable regions due to overlap between reproducible and irreproducible components.
- Reproducible and irreproducible components lack separation, limiting detection in some scenarios (e.g., low r_0 or small pi_1).
- MaRR tends to have lower power for smaller values of the proportion of reproducible metabolites.
