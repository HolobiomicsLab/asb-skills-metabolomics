# sampling-frequency-perturbation-testing

## Summary

A robustness-testing protocol that regenerates synthetic time-course GC-MS metabolomics datasets with reduced sampling frequency (50 to 15 timepoints) and added noise (coefficient of variation 5% and 15%), then re-runs MetaboPAC to quantify how both perturbations degrade inference of absolute concentrations from relative abundance data.

## When to use

Use when a concentration-inference method validated on dense, noiseless time-course metabolomics data must be stress-tested against realistic acquisition conditions — i.e., fewer sampled timepoints and noisy relative-abundance measurements with defined CoV.

## When NOT to use

- The goal is to evaluate inference on noiseless, densely sampled data — use the plain MetaboPAC_*.m workflow instead of the _noisy variant
- The metabolomics data are from eukaryotic multi-compartment systems with transport reactions across intracellular compartments, which MetaboPAC does not model
- Only a single noise level or sampling rate is of interest and no head-to-head comparison of the two perturbations is needed

## Inputs

- Noiseless synthetic time-course datasets generated by GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m (first cell) for the four studied models
- MetaboPAC_*_noisy.m scripts from the gtStyLab/MetaboPAC repository
- Model definitions (stoichiometric matrices, kinetic equations) for determined, underdetermined-with-regulation, S. cerevisiae, and E. coli systems
- True response factors sampled uniformly between 1 and 1000 (20 replicate sets per system)

## Outputs

- Noise-added datasets at timepoints [50 15] with CoV [5 15] (%) and impulse-function-smoothed time courses
- Inference-quality statistics aggregated across 20 repetitions per (model, timepoints, CoV, percKnownKinetics) combination
- Robustness tabulation (e.g., results_noisy_robustness.csv) classifying each model as robust or degraded under each perturbation

## How to apply

First regenerate noiseless in silico data with the first cell of GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m in MATLAB for each model system. Then use the second cell of driver_genDatasets*.m to generate noise-added datasets with timepoints [50 15] and CoV values [5 15] (%), repeating over repetitions [1:20], applying impulse-function smoothing to the noisy time courses. Run MetaboPAC_*_noisy.m for each model over repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics values of [0 20 40 60 80 100], then aggregate inference-quality statistics across the 20 repetitions for every (model, timepoints, CoV) combination. The rationale is that reduced sampling frequency and measurement noise are independent stressors, so tabulating robustness patterns per model (e.g., determined model robust to sampling rate but noise-sensitive; underdetermined-with-regulation model noise-robust but sampling-sensitive) separates the two effects rather than conflating them.

## Related tools

- **MATLAB** (Execution environment for driver_genDatasets*.m (noiseless and noisy dataset generation) and for the MetaboPAC_*_noisy.m robustness runs)
- **MetaboPAC** (The concentration-inference framework under test; its _noisy script variant is re-run across sampling-frequency and CoV conditions) — gtStyLab/MetaboPAC

## Examples

```
matlab -batch "MetaboPAC_model_noisy"  % run per-model _noisy scripts: timepoints [50 15], CoV [5 15], repetitions [1:20], percKnownKinetics [0 20 40 60 80 100]
```

## Evaluation signals

- MetaboPAC achieves significant inference of concentrations in 3 of the 4 studied models under lower sampling frequency and high noise
- The determined synthetic model is robust to decreased sampling rate but sensitive to added noise, while the underdetermined-with-regulation model is more robust to high noise than to reduced sampling frequency
- S. cerevisiae remains robust to both perturbations while E. coli performance is degraded by both — deviations should be recorded in results_noisy_robustness.csv
- Each (model, timepoints, CoV) condition is backed by all 20 repetitions rather than a single run
- percKnownKinetics sweep covers [0 20 40 60 80 100] so performance can be stratified by fraction of known kinetic terms

## Limitations

- High noise levels in the data can violate mass balances and complicate assessment of biological feasibility
- Prediction accuracy varies widely (40% to nearly 100%) depending on network, data quality, and error tolerance
- Missing values degrade accuracy — ~10% missingness with imputation makes inferred concentrations less accurate, so perturbation testing assumes complete time courses
- Response factors were assumed constant with concentration; ion suppression or a limited linear dynamic range may violate this
- MetaboPAC applies only to intracellular, single-compartment metabolomics samples and ignores transport reactions across eukaryotic compartments
