# concentration-inference-significance-assessment

## Summary

Stress-tests MetaboPAC's inference of absolute metabolite concentrations from relative-abundance GC-MS data under realistic noise and reduced sampling frequency, checking whether inference remains statistically significant. It quantifies model-dependent robustness across four in silico metabolic systems using controlled coefficient-of-variation and timepoint degradation.

## When to use

Use when assessing whether inferred absolute concentrations from relative-abundance time-course GC-MS metabolomics data remain significant under realistic experimental conditions — noise (CoV 5–15%) and sparser timepoint sampling (50→15) — rather than on clean simulated data.

## When NOT to use

- Evaluating the baseline noiseless performance or response factors sampled from log-uniform distributions — use MetaboPAC_*.m or MetaboPAC_*_log.m instead, not the _noisy variants
- Applying to multi-compartment eukaryotic metabolomics data with transport reactions between intracellular compartments — MetaboPAC assumes a single compartment and all metabolites directly connected via reactions
- Datasets with substantial missing values — 10% missingness followed by imputation already reduces inferred-concentration accuracy, compounding the noise stress test

## Inputs

- Noiseless in silico datasets generated by the first cell of GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m (relative abundance concMatrix and timeVec)
- Noise-added datasets with reduced sampling frequency (second cell of GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m), impulse-function smoothed
- MetaboPAC_*_noisy.m scripts for each of the four systems (determined, underdetermined-with-regulation, S. cerevisiae, E. coli)
- Randomly generated true response factors from trueRF (20 replicate sets per system)

## Outputs

- Per-(model, timepoints, CoV) aggregated inference-quality statistics across 20 repetitions
- Significance assessment of concentration inference per system (target: significant in 3 of 4 models)
- Tabulated robustness patterns and deviation log (e.g., results_noisy_robustness.csv)

## How to apply

Start from noiseless datasets generated by the first cell of GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m, then produce noise-added datasets with the second cell of that driver using timepoints [50 15], coefficient-of-variation values [5 15] (%), repetitions [1:20], applying impulse-function smoothing to the noisy time courses. Run MetaboPAC_*_noisy.m for each of the four systems over repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics values of [0 20 40 60 80 100]. Aggregate inference-quality statistics across the 20 repetitions for each (model, timepoints, CoV) combination and tabulate robustness patterns: the determined model is robust to reduced sampling rate but noise-sensitive; the underdetermined-with-regulation model is noise-robust but sampling-sensitive; S. cerevisiae is robust to both; E. coli is degraded by both. Validate success by confirming significant inference of concentrations in 3 of the 4 studied models, recording deviations (e.g., to results_noisy_robustness.csv). Rationale: noise can violate the mass balances MetaboPAC relies on for judging biological feasibility, so robustness must be tested per system rather than assumed.

## Examples

```
matlab -batch "run('MetaboPAC_determined_noisy.m')"  % run per system over timepoints [50 15], CoV [5 15], repetitions [1:20]
```

## Evaluation signals

- MetaboPAC achieves significant inference of concentrations in 3 of the 4 studied models under lower sampling frequency and high noise; deviations are recorded in results_noisy_robustness.csv
- Per-model robustness pattern matches expectations: determined model robust to sampling-rate reduction but noise-sensitive; underdetermined-with-regulation model noise-robust but sampling-sensitive; S. cerevisiae robust to both; E. coli degraded by both
- Noise parameters exactly match the manuscript grid: timepoints [50 15], CoV [5 15] (%), repetitions [1:20]
- percKnownKinetics sweep covers [0 20 40 60 80 100] and statistics are aggregated across all 20 repetitions per condition, not single runs
- Impulse-function smoothing was applied to noisy time courses before MetaboPAC execution

## Limitations

- High noise levels in the data can lead to violations of the metabolic mass balances, complicating assessment of biological feasibility
- Multiple sets of response factors had similar or even smaller penalty values than the true ones, suggesting the penalties need refinement
- Prediction accuracy varies widely (40% to nearly 100%) depending on metabolic network, data quality, and error tolerance
- Assumes constant response factors with concentration; linear dynamic range violations or ion suppression may not hold
- Restricted to intracellular metabolomics samples in a single compartment; transport reactions in eukaryotic systems are not modeled
