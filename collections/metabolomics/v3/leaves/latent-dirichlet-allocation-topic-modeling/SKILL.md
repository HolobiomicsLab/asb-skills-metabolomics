# latent-dirichlet-allocation-topic-modeling

## Summary

Adapts latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), a text-mining topic model, to mass spectrometry fragmentation data: MS2 spectra are converted into bag-of-fragments documents and decomposed via variational Bayesian inference into Mass2Motifs — co-occurring fragment/neutral-loss substructures — enabling unsupervised substructure discovery in metabolomics.

## When to use

Use when you have a corpus of MS2 fragmentation spectra (e.g., MGF from GNPS or Mass Bank) and want unsupervised extraction of recurring molecular substructures without prior compound identification, or when testing that a spectral dataset decomposes into a target number of motifs.

## When NOT to use

- Data are simple mixtures with few metabolites or information-poor MS2 spectra (not generated by ramped or stepped collision energy) — the paper states MS2LDA works best on complex mixtures with information-rich spectra
- You need automated spectral matching that includes neutral losses — losses are not supported by spectral matching tools and had to be omitted in annotation
- A fast, simple tool suffices — MS2LDA is computationally more costly than simpler tools (though 'not prohibitively so')

## Inputs

- MGF-format MS/MS spectra (e.g., 5,670 GNPS spectra from the CSI:FingerID study; 1,953 Mass Bank spectra)
- Preprocessed bag-of-fragments spectral documents with neutral losses and noise peaks removed
- Optional fixed motif sets from prior MS2LDA runs (e.g., structurally characterized beer Mass2Motifs)

## Outputs

- Set of Mass2Motifs (e.g., ~500), each a probability distribution over fragments and neutral losses
- Per-spectrum motif loadings (decomposition of each spectrum into motifs)
- motifset.json export plus binary LDA model dump
- metrics.json capturing inferred motif count and elapsed training runtime

## How to apply

First preprocess each MS2 spectrum with the MS2LDA preprocessing module: convert it into a bag-of-fragments representation, extract neutral losses, and filter out noise peaks. Then run MS2LDA model training with the number of Mass2Motifs fixed (the paper decomposed the 5,670-spectrum GNPS CSI:FingerID dataset and 1,953 Mass Bank spectra into 500 motifs each, using online variational Bayesian inference). Optionally keep previously characterized motifs (e.g., motifs characterized from beer positive-mode files) fixed while all other motifs are learned during inference. After training converges, count the inferred Mass2Motifs, record runtime, and export the motif set (motifset.json) with per-spectrum motif loadings and the LDA model dump. Rationale: LDA treats fragments/losses as words and spectra as documents, so motifs emerge as sets of co-occurring fragments and neutral losses that can then be subjected to spectral matching (e.g., against Mass Bank/NIST at a 90% normalized score threshold) for annotation.

## Evaluation signals

- The decomposition of the 5,670-spectrum GNPS dataset yields approximately 500 Mass2Motifs, matching the paper's reported result (similarly ~500 motifs from 1,953 Mass Bank spectra)
- metrics.json records the expected inferred motif count and a finite elapsed training runtime (training converges via online variational inference)
- motifset.json parses and contains per-spectrum motif loadings alongside the binary LDA model dump
- Extracted motifs are biochemically plausible: motif degree (number of spectra containing a motif) spans a wide range, from 1 to over 200 spectra as in the paper
- Motif spectra subjected to spectral matching against Mass Bank/NIST at a 90% normalized score threshold, or substructure annotations at a probability threshold of 0.1, reproduce validation rates on characterized positive-mode beer motifs (19 of 19 correctly annotated)

## Limitations

- MS2LDA works best only for complex mixtures with information-rich MS2 spectra (e.g., generated by ramped or stepped collision energy)
- Neutral losses are not supported by current spectral matching tools and had to be omitted in automated annotation of Mass2Motifs
- Computationally more costly than simpler tools
- Automated Mass2Motif annotation was validated only on characterized positive-mode beer motifs; performance in other ionization modes/matrices is not reported
