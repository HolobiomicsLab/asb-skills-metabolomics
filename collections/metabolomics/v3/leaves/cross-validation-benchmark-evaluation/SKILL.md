# cross-validation-benchmark-evaluation

## Summary

Benchmark evaluation of MSNovelist's de novo structure ranking on MS2 spectra, including the ablation that ranks RNN-generated candidates by raw decoder sequence probability instead of the modified Platt fingerprint-match re-ranking, across 10 cross-validation folds.

## When to use

Use when quantifying top-1 and overall retrieval rates of de novo structure predictions on MS2 spectra (e.g. GNPS, GNPS-OK), or when ablating the modified Platt re-ranking step to isolate the RNN's raw ranking power.

## When NOT to use

- The goal is the best possible identification rate rather than an ablation — the article states final re-ranking with the modified Platt score is important for best results, so skipping it degrades performance by design
- Spectra are negative-mode MS2 — MSNovelist is only trained for positive-mode data, so benchmark numbers will not transfer
- Spectra have fewer than three fragment ions or were processed with a SIRIUS version other than the workspace format MSNovelist expects (SIRIUS 4.4.29 for this repo)

## Inputs

- Query MS2 spectra (GNPS and GNPS-OK benchmark sets)
- SIRIUS-predicted molecular formulas per spectrum
- CSI:FingerID-predicted structural fingerprints per spectrum
- k validated and dereplicated candidate SMILES from the encoder–decoder RNN
- Reference structure (SMILES) per query spectrum
- Raw decoder sequence probabilities and modified Platt scores for each candidate

## Outputs

- Top-1 identification rates per dataset (ablation: 19% on GNPS, 39% on GNPS-OK)
- Overall retrieval rates per dataset
- Median modified Platt score of best RNN-alone candidates (reported: −5,910)
- Median Tanimoto similarity of best incorrect de novo candidates to the reference (reported: 0.74)
- Per-fold evaluation results across the 10 cross-validation folds

## How to apply

Launch the MSNovelist Singularity container (built as msnovelist.sif from docker://stravsm/msnovelist6) with run_evaluation.sh/evaluation.py via 'sbatch --array=0-9' so each of the 10 cross-validation folds is evaluated in a CPU-only SLURM job. For each query MS2 spectrum, take the k validated and dereplicated candidate SMILES generated by the encoder–decoder RNN from the SIRIUS-predicted formula and CSI:FingerID-predicted fingerprint. For the ablation, rank candidates by raw decoder sequence probability, deliberately skipping the modified Platt fingerprint-match re-ranking that the default pipeline applies. Compare the top-ranked SMILES against the reference structure per spectrum to compute top-1 and overall retrieval rates on GNPS and GNPS-OK, and additionally compute Tanimoto similarities between predicted and reference fingerprints for the best incorrect de novo candidates plus Platt-score statistics of the rankings. The rationale is that the default pipeline re-ranks by fingerprint match, so ablating it reveals how much ranking quality comes from the RNN's own sequence probability versus the Platt scoring.

## Related tools

- **MSNovelist** (Containerized evaluation pipeline whose encoder–decoder RNN candidates are ranked by raw sequence probability for the ablation benchmark) — https://github.com/meowcat/MSNovelist
- **SIRIUS** (Predicts the molecular formula xM that constrains candidate generation for each benchmark spectrum) — https://bio.informatik.uni-jena.de/software/sirius/

## Examples

```

```

## Evaluation signals

- Raw-probability top-1 rates reproduce 19% on GNPS and 39% on GNPS-OK (versus 25% top-1 / 45% retrieval with Platt re-ranking)
- Best RNN-alone candidates have median modified Platt score of −5,910
- Best incorrect de novo candidates have median Tanimoto similarity of 0.74 in this ablation (0.80 in the full pipeline)
- All 10 cross-validation folds complete as CPU-only SLURM array jobs
- Result records expose score_mod_platt and rank fields so top candidates can be cross-checked against the reported rankings

## Limitations

- Skipping modified Platt re-ranking reduces top-1 rate from 25% to 19% on GNPS, so the ablation should not be used as the production ranking
- MSNovelist is trained only for positive-mode data; negative-mode benchmark performance is expected to be lower
- Formula determination error rates rise to >50% for individual spectra up to m/z 800, capping attainable benchmark scores
