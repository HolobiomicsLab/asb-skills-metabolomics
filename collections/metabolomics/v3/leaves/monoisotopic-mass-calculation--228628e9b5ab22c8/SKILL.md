# monoisotopic-mass-calculation

## Summary

Compute monoisotopic masses for fragment formulae (and hydrogen-tolerant formula variants) generated by combinatorial bond breaking, so that predicted fragment distributions can be converted into an MS/MS spectrum by matching formula masses to peak m/z values.

## When to use

Use when a fragment set from a bond-breaking DAG must be converted to predicted peak masses — enumerate formulae {f_n^{-j},...,f_n^{j}} per fragment and compute exact monoisotopic masses for spectrum construction or formula annotation.

## When NOT to use

- The candidate formulae do not originate from a bond-breaking fragment DAG — e.g. GrAFF-MS instead selects the top 10,000 product and neutral loss formulae by total intensity across the dataset.
- The task is fragment identity prediction or peak explanation for chemistry outside the model's scope (e.g. cyclizations), where unexplained OS peaks arise that the mass set cannot account for.
- The analysis targets glycan or peptide spectra, which the paper explicitly excluded from its formula annotation analysis to focus on small molecules.

## Inputs

- Molecular structure parsed with RDKit into a molecular graph G=(V,E)
- Fragmentation DAG G_F^d from recursive bond-breaking (d=3 or d=4), with per-node fragment subgraph G_n, heavy-atom formula f_n, and attached hydrogen count h_n
- Observed MS/MS peak masses for annotation matching (e.g. from NIST20 MS/MS spectra)

## Outputs

- Hydrogen-tolerant formulae {f_n^{-j},...,f_n^{j}} per fragment with tolerance j=4
- Monoisotopic masses {m_n^{-j},...,m_n^{j}} for each formula variant (e.g. mass(C2O1H5)=45.03404 Da)
- Derived mass set M_hat(G,d,j) used to convert fragment distributions into predicted MS/MS spectra
- Serialized DAG nodes annotated with formula and mass values (JSON graph files)

## How to apply

First parse the molecule with RDKit into a molecular graph and strip hydrogens to obtain the heavy atom skeleton H(G), since |H2(G)| is on average ~43% smaller than |E| on NIST20. For each fragment node n in the fragmentation DAG (generated by recursive edge removal to depth d = 3 or 4), record its subgraph G_n, heavy-atom molecular formula f_n, and attached hydrogen count h_n. Enumerate the hydrogen-tolerant formulae {f_n^{-j},...,f_n^{j}} with tolerance j = 4 and compute each monoisotopic exact mass, e.g. mass(C2O1H5) = 45.03404 Da. The resulting mass set M_hat(G,d,j) converts a predicted fragment distribution into an MS/MS spectrum, and derived masses are matched against observed peaks with a 10 ppm tolerance when formula annotations are evaluated. Validation should confirm the mass set with d in {3,4} and j=4 captures most of the total peak intensity in real MS/MS data.

## Related tools

- **RDKit** (Parses input molecular structures into molecular graphs and computes the heavy atom skeleton from which fragment formulae f_n are derived)
- **Cython** (Hosts the implementation of the recursive fragmentation algorithm that produces the DAG nodes whose formulae get mass annotations) — github.com/FraGNNet/fragnnet
- **Python** (Language in which the FraGNNet model, baselines, and mass/formula bookkeeping are implemented) — github.com/FraGNNet/fragnnet
- **NumPy** (Numerical backend for computing and comparing monoisotopic formula masses against peak masses)

## Evaluation signals

- Spot-check exact masses against reference values, e.g. mass(C2O1H5) must equal 45.03404 Da.
- Structural invariants of the underlying DAG: root corresponds to the original graph, leaves correspond to individual atoms, and every edge is a single-bond-removal parent-child relation.
- Formula annotations are rejected if their formula masses lie more than 10 ppm from the associated peak mass.
- Coverage check: M_hat(G,d,j) computed with d in {3,4} and j=4 should capture most of the total peak intensity in real MS/MS data.
- Hydrogen-tolerance enumeration stays within the specified bound j=4 variants around each heavy-atom formula f_n.

## Limitations

- The algorithm fails to account for some fragments, evidenced by unexplained Outside-of-Support (OS) peaks in spectra.
- Unexplained peaks may arise from complex chemistry such as cyclizations, which dramatically expand the fragment search space and are challenging to model.
- Fragmentation depends on recursive edge-removal operations that are not parallelized, limiting runtime.
- Performance was evaluated on merged spectra from an Orbitrap instrument with HCD and [M+H]+ adducts; broader instrument platforms and precursor adducts were not covered.
