# welch-two-sample-ttest-adduct-abundance-comparison

## Summary

Statistical validation of Ion Identity Molecular Networking (IIMN) adduct annotations by comparing [M+Na]+ and [M+NH4]+ feature abundances across salt-infusion versus control conditions using Welch two-sample t-tests.

## When to use

Use when validating ion-identity/adduct assignments from an aligned MS1 feature table across experimental conditions where a specific adduct class should change in abundance (e.g. post-column salt infusion of sodium acetate or ammonium acetate vs H2O control).

## When NOT to use

- Input is raw spectral data or an unaligned feature list — grouping, ion-identity annotation and feature-height extraction must be completed first (e.g. via MZmine IIMN) before the t-test is meaningful.
- Comparing ion species whose abundance should NOT differ across conditions (e.g. isotopes of the same molecule within one sample group) — the test targets condition-induced adduct shifts, not within-run annotation.
- Datasets with heavily missing or imputed feature heights where the normality/variance assumptions of a t-test are indefensible; use non-parametric alternatives instead.

## Inputs

- Aligned MS1 feature quantification table (.csv) from MZmine with per-sample feature heights
- Ion identity / adduct annotation per feature (e.g. [M+Na]+, [M+NH4]+) from the IIMN/IIN grouping
- Sample-to-condition mapping (control H2O infusion vs Na-acetate infusion vs NH4-acetate infusion)

## Outputs

- Per-adduct mean abundances by condition
- Fold change (salt infusion vs control) per adduct class
- Welch t-statistic and p-value per comparison
- Summary table of significant adduct abundance shifts (p < 0.001 expected in the benchmark)

## How to apply

Start from the ion-identity annotated feature quantification table exported by MZmine (feature heights per sample, with adduct annotations such as [M+Na]+ and [M+NH4]+ generated by the IIMN workflow). Group features by adduct annotation and infusion condition (control H2O infusion vs Na-acetate vs NH4-acetate post-column infusion). For each adduct, run a Welch two-sample t-test (unequal variances) comparing abundance under the corresponding salt infusion against control — e.g. [M+Na]+ abundances under Na-acetate infusion vs control, and [M+NH4]+ abundances under NH4-acetate infusion vs control. Tabulate per-adduct mean abundances, fold changes, t-statistics and p-values. The expected outcome is significantly increased abundance (p < 0.001 in the article's salt-infusion benchmark) of [M+Na]+ under sodium acetate and [M+NH4]+ under ammonium acetate, confirming that the IIMN ion-species identification reflects induced adduct formation. The unequal-variance Welch test is used because feature abundances across infusion conditions may have different spreads and the sample counts per condition are modest.

## Examples

```
t.test(height ~ infusion_condition, data = subset(features, adduct == '[M+Na]+'), var.equal = FALSE)
```

## Evaluation signals

- [M+Na]+ mean abundance under Na-acetate infusion is significantly higher than control with Welch p < 0.001 (article reports p < 0.001)
- [M+NH4]+ mean abundance under NH4-acetate infusion is significantly higher than control with Welch p < 0.001
- Fold changes are computed as mean abundance (salt infusion) / mean abundance (control) and are > 1 for the matched adduct class
- Test is two-sample Welch (unequal variances), not pooled Student's t — verify via the degrees of freedom / unequal-variance implementation used
- Per-adduct comparisons are limited to features sharing the same ion identity annotation, so no mixing of [M+Na]+ and [M+NH4]+ abundances occurs within a single test

## Limitations

- Statistical significance depends on the induced-adduct ground truth design (post-column salt infusion); on natural abundance data without a condition that selectively alters adduct formation, the test has no expected effect and p-values are uninformative.
- Only paired adduct-vs-control contrasts for the specific salt used (Na-acetate for [M+Na]+, NH4-acetate for [M+NH4]+) are validated; cross-comparisons (e.g. [M+Na]+ under NH4-acetate) are not part of the article's validation.
- Sample size per condition is small in the benchmark, so the t-test's power and normality assumption should be checked before over-interpreting borderline p-values.
- Feature heights must be extracted consistently (same peak-shape/height parameters in MZmine) across all conditions or abundance shifts could reflect processing artifacts rather than chemistry.
