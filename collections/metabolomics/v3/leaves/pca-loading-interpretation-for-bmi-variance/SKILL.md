# pca-loading-interpretation-for-bmi-variance

## Summary

Applies PCA to a matrix of simulated 1D 1H NMR spectra (e.g. 100 blood-mixture replicates generated by MetAssimulo 2.0 against a continuous BMI outcome) and interprets which principal component — here PC2 — carries the BMI-related variance, using explained variance and loading-vector inspection to identify the driving metabolite (lactate).

## When to use

Use when you have simulated or measured 1D 1H NMR spectra of blood mixtures tied to a continuous outcome such as BMI, and need to determine which principal component captures the outcome-related variance and which metabolites drive it.

## When NOT to use

- When the spectra are raw urine or CSF simulations without a blood protein background, since the reported PC2/BMI relationship and ~0.82 Pearson correlation were established for blood spectra.
- When the outcome of interest is a discrete case–control label (e.g. heart transplant versus healthy) rather than a continuous variable like BMI — the PC2-loading interpretation described here is specific to a continuous outcome.
- When the goal is spectral generation itself rather than downstream dimensionality reduction — use MetAssimulo 2.0 alone for simulation and this skill only for the PCA step.

## Inputs

- Simulated 1D 1H NMR blood spectra matrix (~100 replicates) generated by MetAssimulo 2.0 from the 10 BMI-correlated metabolites, with inter-metabolite correlations and the broad protein background
- Continuous BMI outcome values associated with each replicate (from the section 2.2.2 linear regression model)

## Outputs

- PC scores matrix (100 samples x retained components)
- Explained variance per PC (PC1–PC5)
- PC2 loading vector across spectral buckets/ppm variables, with lactate expected as the top absolute loading
- Summary CSV (e.g. simulated_bmi_spectra_pca.csv) containing spectra, scores, loadings, and the PC2/BMI conclusion

## How to apply

Generate or obtain a spectra matrix of ~100 replicates (e.g. MetAssimulo 2.0 blood spectra that include the broad protein background, Pearson correlation ~0.82 with real spectra), each replicate containing metabolites correlated with the BMI outcome via the section 2.2.2 linear-regression concentration model. Export the 100-sample spectra matrix together with its BMI values, then run PCA (e.g. scikit-learn PCA with centering/scaling of spectral buckets) to obtain PC scores, explained variance, and loadings. Inspect PC1–PC5 explained variance and the loading vectors to confirm that PC2 captures most of the BMI-related variance and that the lactate bucket has the largest absolute PC2 loading, matching the reported section 2.2.2 finding. Save the spectra, scores, loadings, and a short summary (e.g. simulated_bmi_spectra_pca.csv) so the PC2-vs-BMI association and the top-loading metabolite can be verified later.

## Examples

```
export PYTHONPATH=$HOME/MetAssimulo_2/:$PYTHONPATH; python3 apps/index.py -p Input/parameters.txt; python3 -c "import pandas as pd; from sklearn.decomposition import PCA; X=pd.read_csv('simulated_bmi_spectra.csv'); p=PCA(n_components=5); p.fit(X.drop(columns=['BMI'])); print(p.explained_variance_ratio_, p.components_[1])"
```

## Evaluation signals

- The PCA summary shows PC2 with a substantially higher BMI association than PC1–PC5, as reported in section 2.2.2.
- The lactate spectral bucket has the largest absolute PC2 loading across all buckets.
- The simulated blood spectra correlate with real blood spectra at approximately 0.82 Pearson correlation before PCA is applied.
- The output CSV contains all three artefacts (spectra, scores, loadings) plus a summary line stating PC2 captures most BMI-related variance.
- The spectra matrix has the expected dimensions (100 samples x spectral buckets) and is paired with BMI values for every replicate.

## Limitations

- The skill is validated on MetAssimulo 2.0-simulated blood spectra with a broad protein background; real blood spectra may deviate.
- The ~0.82 Pearson correlation between simulated and real blood spectra is the article's reported approximation, not a guarantee for other sample types.
- Inter-metabolite correlations must be enabled during simulation to reproduce the section 2.2.2 findings; disabling them changes the loading structure.
