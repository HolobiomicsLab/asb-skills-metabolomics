# msms-spectrum-deconvolution-export

## Summary

Deconvolute and export MS/MS spectra from untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics runs (HILIC- and CSH-Q Exactive HF) into .mgf format so they can be batch-searched against spectral databases such as NIST17 for structure annotation.

## When to use

Use when deconvoluted MS/MS peak lists from LC-MS/MS metabolomics data (e.g. Q Exactive HF acquisitions) must be exported as .mgf files to feed downstream spectral library searches such as the NIST17 hybrid search.

## When NOT to use

- The input is already an annotated compound table or feature table with MSI levels assigned — no spectral export is needed
- Only MSI level 1/2 annotations by authentic standards or precursor accurate mass are targeted, without spectral library search
- The spectra are raw centroided vendor files that have not yet been deconvoluted into per-precursor MS/MS peak lists

## Inputs

- Deconvoluted MS/MS spectra from lipidomics and HILIC-Q Exactive HF runs of urine samples (data-dependent acquisition)
- Precursor accurate masses and associated retention times from feature detection

## Outputs

- .mgf-format file(s) of deconvoluted MS/MS spectra ready for batch spectral search against NIST17

## How to apply

Start from the deconvoluted MS/MS spectra generated by the untargeted metabolomics assays (HILIC-Q Exactive HF and CSH-Q Exactive HF); in the IC urine study, over 9,000 unique metabolite signals were detected and 42% triggered MS/MS fragmentations in data-dependent mode, and only these deconvoluted spectra are exported. Write each deconvoluted MS/MS spectrum with its precursor mass into .mgf format, which is the input format expected by batch spectral search engines. The exported .mgf files then serve as input to the NIST17 hybrid search (which combines in-silico predicted fragments with library spectra) to assign MSI level 3 structure annotations, or to in-silico tools such as SIRIUS/CSI:FingerID for fragmentation-tree-based annotation. Correctness is judged downstream: the search should reach approximately 95% spectral assignment coverage, confirming that the export preserved precursor masses and fragment peak lists faithfully.

## Related tools

- **SIRIUS** (java-based framework for analysis of LC-MS/MS data of metabolites; consumes exported MS/MS spectra for fragmentation tree and CSI:FingerID analysis) — https://github.com/sirius-ms/sirius
- **NIST17 hybrid search** (downstream consumer of the exported .mgf spectra for batch hybrid search annotation at MSI level 3)
- **PySirius / RSirius SDK** (OpenAPI client SDKs for programmatically driving SIRIUS processing of exported spectra) — https://github.com/sirius-ms/sirius-client-openAPI

## Evaluation signals

- Every exported .mgf entry carries a precursor mass consistent with the deconvoluted feature's accurate mass
- The fraction of MS/MS-triggered features represented in the export matches the expected ~42% of detected signals (9,000+ unique signals in the urine study)
- Downstream NIST17 hybrid search of the exported spectra assigns structure annotations to approximately 95% of spectra within ~10 minutes of processing
- ClassyFire classification of the resulting annotations yields 21 superclasses / 184 chemical classes with top classes 28% derivatives of organic acids, 16% heterocyclics, 16% lipids

## Limitations

- Many structure assignments from downstream searches still require validation with authentic chemicals; the export step itself guarantees nothing about annotation confidence
- The majority of acquired MS/MS spectra cannot be annotated with certainty to specific chemicals, so high export coverage does not equal high-confidence identification
- MSI level 3 class distributions can differ across cohorts due to diet and chemical exposure, so exported-spectrum-derived annotations are population-dependent
