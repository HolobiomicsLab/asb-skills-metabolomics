# metabolite-candidate-reranking

## Summary

Ranks and filters metabolite candidate lists from tools like MS-Finder by predicting liquid chromatography retention times with the RT-Transformer model (graph attention network + 1D-Transformer) and comparing predictions to measured RTs under the chromatographic method used.

## When to use

Use when a non-targeted metabolomics workflow has produced per-spectrum candidate compound lists (e.g. MS-Finder outputs) plus the measured retention time of the true metabolite, and you need to filter or rerank candidates by RT agreement.

## When NOT to use

- When no measured retention time is available for the spectrum or the chromatographic method differs from the one the model was fine-tuned on — RT deviation thresholds and tolerances are condition-specific and will not generalize without retraining.
- When the goal is de novo metabolite identification from spectra alone without candidate structures — this skill reranks existing MS-Finder candidate lists and does not generate candidates.
- When candidates are already annotated with experimental RTs under the same method — direct RT matching is preferable to model-based prediction.

## Inputs

- Per-spectrum candidate compound lists generated by MS-Finder (with candidate structures, e.g. InChI)
- Measured retention time of the true metabolite for each spectrum under the specific LC chromatographic method
- Transfer-learned RT-Transformer model (pre-trained checkpoint, e.g. best_state_dict.pth or best_state_download_dict.pth, fine-tuned on the target chromatographic condition)

## Outputs

- Filtered and reranked candidate list per spectrum, ordered by predicted RT deviation from the measured RT
- Counts of correctly identified candidates and erroneously filtered true candidates across all spectra
- Average ranking improvement of the correct compound (in positions) for annotations outside the top 10
- Overall fraction of candidate compounds filtered across all spectra (e.g. 52.64% in the Metabobase benchmark)

## How to apply

For each spectrum, take the MS-Finder candidate compound list and the measured retention time of the true metabolite, predict an RT for every candidate using the transfer-learned RT-Transformer model (pre-trained on the 80 038-molecule SMRT dataset, then fine-tuned on the target chromatographic condition, e.g. via 10-fold cross-validation on Metabobase data from PredRet/MoNa), and discard candidates whose predicted RT deviates from the measured RT beyond the model-derived tolerance window. Rerank the surviving candidates by the magnitude of RT deviation and record how the rank of the correct compound changes relative to its original MS-Finder position. Aggregate across spectra: count correctly identified candidates, count true candidates erroneously filtered, compute the average rank improvement, and compute the overall fraction of candidates filtered. In the Metabobase benchmark this yielded 93 correctly identified compounds, 10 erroneously filtered, an average improvement of 11 ranking positions for correct annotations outside the top 10, and 52.64% of candidates filtered across all spectra.


## Evaluation signals

- Correctly identified candidate count matches the benchmark (93 for Metabobase with MS-Finder candidates).
- Number of true candidates erroneously filtered matches the benchmark (10 for Metabobase).
- Average rank improvement of correct annotations outside the top 10 matches the benchmark (11 positions).
- Overall fraction of candidates filtered across all spectra matches the benchmark (52.64%).
- Per-spectrum output preserves all surviving candidates, reranked strictly by ascending absolute RT deviation from the measured RT.

## Limitations

- RT tolerance windows are derived from the fine-tuned model and are specific to a chromatographic method; transferring to a new condition requires fine-tuning (e.g. transfer.py on a csv with 'InChI' and 'RT' columns).
- Molecules that are 'not retained' inflate error metrics — the paper reports average absolute error of 27.30 after removing not retained molecules vs 33.41 when no samples are removed.
- Filtering can erroneously discard the true metabolite (10 compounds in the Metabobase benchmark), so the filter is a trade-off between candidate reduction and recall.
