# Early-stopping hyperparameter tuning

## Summary

Trains ROASMI's RankNet retention-order module on pairwise elution probabilities using the chemprop D-MPNN embeddings, tuning hyperparameters per the paper's Table 1 and applying early stopping on validation pairwise loss to prevent overfitting.

## When to use

Use when training a neural ranking/retention model from molecular embeddings where a pairwise binary cross-entropy loss must be tuned and monitored to avoid overfitting on limited reference retention data.

## When NOT to use

- When the input is already a set of predicted retention scores rather than a pairwise elution-order training problem — use ROASMI's ROASMI_predict.py instead.
- When the chromatographic system is HILIC rather than RPLC; the paper states that constructing retention models with generalizability under HILIC remains future work.
- When the task is to distinguish coexisting isomers or integrate retention scores with MS/MS scores (Identify.py); those are downstream skills, not the training step.

## Inputs

- Reference retention dataset of molecular pairs with known elution-order labels (e.g., dataset_87 for RPLC at eluent pH ~2.7)
- D-MPNN molecular embeddings generated by chemprop from compound SMILES
- Hyperparameter values from the paper's Table 1 (learning rate, batch size, hidden dimensions, message-passing steps, dropout)

## Outputs

- Trained RankNet ensemble members ROASMI_1 through ROASMI_5 (saved model checkpoints)
- Pairwise elution probability P_ij with cross-seed mean and variance for held-out molecular pairs

## How to apply

Load the reference retention dataset (e.g., dataset_87 for RPLC) and split it into train/validation/test partitions at an 8:1:1 ratio. Implement the RankNet loss in PyTorch, which computes a binary cross-entropy between the sigmoid of the predicted pairwise retention scores P_ij — the probability that compound Ci elutes before Cj — and the observed elution-order label; use chemprop D-MPNN molecular embeddings as input features so the model can generalize to new chemical spaces. Optimize with the Adam optimizer, setting hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, hidden-layer dimensions, number of message-passing steps, dropout) as reported in the paper's Table 1, and monitor the validation pairwise loss at each epoch, stopping training when that loss stops improving. Repeat the full training procedure under 5 random seeds to produce an ensemble ROASMI_1 through ROASMI_5, whose cross-seed prediction variance serves as the model's uncertainty estimate.

## Evaluation signals

- The trained ensemble must output sigmoid-based P_ij probabilities that a compound elutes before its paired compound, bounded in [0, 1].
- Distinguishable molecular pairs should yield low cross-seed prediction variance, while indistinguishable pairs (e.g., 4-hydroxybenzoic acid vs. 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid) should show high variance (the paper reports e.g. 9% variance for a distinguishable pair).
- Validation pairwise loss must decrease during training and early stopping must be triggered when it plateaus, not when training loss alone plateaus.
- Held-out pairwise elution-order accuracy should reflect the generalizability demonstrated across the paper's 71 independent RPLC datasets.
- All 5 ensemble seeds must converge on comparable retention scores; large cross-seed divergence on training-set pairs indicates the hyperparameters need retuning.

## Limitations

- ROASMI's RankNet cannot distinguish molecular pairs whose chromatographic peaks are extremely close (e.g., 4-hydroxybenzoic acid and 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid), yielding high prediction variance for those pairs.
- The trained model applies only to reversed-phase liquid chromatography (RPLC) systems with an eluent pH around 2.7; no HILIC model is currently available.
- The model is restricted to conventional compounds within elemental restrictions of C, H, N, O, P, and S; broader chemical space remains unexplored.
- RT outliers caused by slight changes in experimental conditions are not resolved and require further mechanistic study.
- GPU acceleration requires CUDA >= 8.0 and cuDNN; CPU-only training may be slow on large reference datasets.
