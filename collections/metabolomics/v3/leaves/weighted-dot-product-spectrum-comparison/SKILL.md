# weighted-dot-product-spectrum-comparison

## Summary

Compute the weighted dot product (WDP) score between observed and theoretical fragment ion intensity vectors to quantitatively evaluate peptide-spectrum matches (PSMs) in Aerith's SIP proteomics workflow. Aerith's results show WDP outperforms MVH, Xcorr, and spectral entropy for scoring SIP-labeled PSMs.

## When to use

Use when scoring peptide-spectrum matches (PSMs) against simulated theoretical spectra of unlabeled or SIP-labeled peptides — e.g., validating Sipros database-search outputs in stable isotope probing (SIP) proteomics.

## When NOT to use

- The goal is simulating isotopic fine structures of metabolites at natural abundance — use Aerith's Monte Carlo/FFT envelope-simulation functions instead of PSM scoring
- Input is an unlabeled metabolomics spectrum without a candidate peptide sequence and theoretical b/y ion series
- PSM scores already exist from a database search (e.g., Sipros output) and only isotope enrichment visualization/annotation is needed

## Inputs

- Observed MS/MS spectrum scan (e.g., .FT2 scan extracted by Raxport from Thermo RAW files)
- Theoretical spectrum for the peptide sequence, with b/y ion series isotopic envelopes simulated by Aerith
- Matched peak pairs (observed fragment peaks aligned to theoretical b/y ions)

## Outputs

- Per-PSM WDP score
- Companion scores (MVH, Xcorr, spectral entropy) for the same PSM
- Per-spectrum score table written to psm_scores.csv

## How to apply

Load the matched observed MS2 scan (e.g., extracted from Thermo RAW via Raxport into .FT2) and the theoretical spectrum generated by Aerith from the peptide sequence, whose fragment ion isotopic distributions are modeled by sequentially convoluting isotopic envelopes of monomeric units. Match observed fragment peaks to the theoretical b/y ion series and compute the WDP as a dot product weighted by the intensities of the matched observed and theoretical peaks. Compute the companion scores — MVH from the count of matched peaks, Xcorr by cross-correlating the observed spectrum against the theoretical spectrum over flanking regions with offset correction, and spectral entropy similarity from transformed peak intensities — so WDP can be benchmarked against them. The algorithms run in C++ accessed via Rcpp for real-time interactivity. Assemble the four scores into a per-spectrum score table (psm_scores.csv); for SIP-labeled PSMs, weight WDP most heavily since the paper found it outperforms the other functions.

## Evaluation signals

- The WDP score is derived from the intensity vectors of matched observed and theoretical fragment peaks, so matched-peak alignment must precede scoring
- Four scores (MVH, Xcorr, WDP, spectral entropy) are assembled per spectrum; a run missing any of the four indicates incomplete application
- For SIP-labeled PSMs, WDP should be the best-discriminating score — compare its ranking behavior against MVH and Xcorr on labeled data
- Score table output should be a per-spectrum table (psm_scores.csv) with one row per PSM
- Theoretical spectra must come from Aerith's sequential convolution of monomeric-unit isotopic envelopes, so isotope-labeled peptides must use user-defined isotopic abundances of the SIP-labeled element

## Limitations

- The FFT-based envelope algorithm does not account for isotopic fine structure, which limits the resolution of the theoretical spectra underlying the scores
- Other proteomic SIP tools (Sipros, MetaProSIP, Calisp) only report estimated labeling percentages, so WDP scores are specific to Aerith's validation pipeline
- WDP's advantage was demonstrated for SIP-labeled PSMs; relative performance against MVH/Xcorr for unlabeled PSMs may differ
