# tanimoto-fingerprint-similarity-evaluation

## Summary

Quantifies how chemically close predicted or de novo–generated structures are to a reference by computing Tanimoto similarity between their molecular fingerprints. In MSNovelist this characterizes the best incorrect de novo candidates against training-set and database-search baselines.

## When to use

Use when evaluating de novo or database-search structure predictions by structural similarity — e.g., computing Tanimoto similarity of top incorrect candidates to the true structure in MS2-based structure elucidation benchmarks.

## When NOT to use

- Isomeric compounds whose fragmentation is indistinguishable — a high Tanimoto similarity cannot resolve them and structural elucidation from the MS2 data is not possible
- The goal is exact identification accuracy (top-1/overall retrieval rates) rather than characterizing near-miss chemical similarity of incorrect candidates
- Fingerprints come from a different prediction system than the one the model was trained on (e.g., the experimental Morgan 4096-bit 'mist' branch work, which was not completed)

## Inputs

- Reference structure SMILES for each query MS2 spectrum
- Candidate structure SMILES generated by the MSNovelist encoder–decoder RNN (validated and dereplicated)
- Structural fingerprints predicted from MS2 spectra by CSI:FingerID

## Outputs

- Per-spectrum Tanimoto similarity between candidate and reference fingerprints
- Median Tanimoto similarity distribution for best incorrect de novo candidates (expected ~0.80 on GNPS)
- Baseline similarity comparisons: best-in-training-set (0.76) and best incorrect database structure (0.84)

## How to apply

Obtain the reference structure SMILES per query MS2 spectrum and the candidate structures (e.g., the k validated and dereplicated SMILES generated by the MSNovelist encoder–decoder RNN from the SIRIUS formula and CSI:FingerID fingerprint). Convert each structure to its molecular fingerprint and compute the pairwise Tanimoto similarity between candidate and reference fingerprints. For evaluation, restrict the similarity analysis to the best-ranked incorrect de novo candidates and report the median Tanimoto (0.80 on GNPS), comparing against the best-in-training-set baseline (0.76) and the best incorrect database structure (0.84); in the RNN-alone (no modified Platt re-ranking) ablation, report the median chemical similarity of 0.74 for the best RNN-alone candidates alongside their median modified Platt score of −5,910. The rationale is that fingerprint predictions from CSI:FingerID encode structural information directly, so high Tanimoto of wrong answers indicates the generator lands near the true chemical neighborhood even when the exact structure is missed.

## Related tools

- **MSNovelist** (Generates the de novo candidate SMILES structures whose fingerprints are compared to the reference via Tanimoto similarity) — https://github.com/meowcat/MSNovelist
- **CSI:FingerID** (Predicts the structural fingerprint from the MS2 spectrum that serves as the input blueprint and the basis of the fingerprint-match score)
- **SIRIUS** (Predicts the molecular formula constraint under which candidate structures are generated before similarity evaluation) — https://bio.informatik.uni-jena.de/software/sirius/

## Evaluation signals

- Median Tanimoto of best incorrect de novo predictions should be ≈0.80 on the GNPS dataset
- Baseline order must hold: de novo incorrect (0.80) > best-in-training-set (0.76) and < best incorrect database structure (0.84)
- For the raw RNN-score-only ablation, the best RNN-alone candidates show median chemical similarity 0.74 with median modified Platt score −5,910
- Similarity values are bounded in [0,1]; a large share of spectra with similarity ≈1.0 but top-1 rank ≠ 1 signals indistinguishable-isomer cases

## Limitations

- High Tanimoto similarity of incorrect structures means the metric reflects chemical-neighborhood proximity, not exact identification; incorrect high-scoring structures were 'highly similar to the correct answers'
- Certain isomers fragment almost indistinguishably, so structural elucidation from MS2 data is not possible in all cases regardless of similarity score
- The generative model's chemical space is conservative, which may limit its ability to propose structures very different from known molecules

## Evidence

- [abstract] Median Tanimoto of best incorrect de novo candidates: "The best incorrect de novo predictions (median 0.80) scored higher than the best-in-training set (0.76) and nearly as high as the best incorrect database structure (0.84)"
- [discussion] Fingerprints as structural blueprint: "structural fingerprint predictions from MS2 spectra (by CSI:FingerID) directly encode structural information and may act as a blueprint for a molecule"
- [discussion] Incorrect predictions are structurally near-misses: "incorrect high-scoring structures were highly similar to the correct answers, both anecdotally and by objective metric."
