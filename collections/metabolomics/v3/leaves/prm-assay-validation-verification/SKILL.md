# prm-assay-validation-verification

## Summary

Validates LipidCreator-generated target-decoy PRM/MRM lipidomics assays by computing fragment-count-dependent match probabilities and comparing computed accuracy ratios against reported target-decoy identification counts. Ensures that a chosen number of fragments (and polarity modes) yields unambiguous lipid species identification.

## When to use

Use when designing or auditing a targeted lipidomics PRM/SRM assay and you must decide how many MS/MS fragments per lipid are needed for unambiguous identification within a background lipidome.

## When NOT to use

- LipidCreator intentionally does not address false discovery rate scoring, coeluting/isobaric lipids, or peak area integration — do not use this skill as a replacement for a dedicated FDR scoring algorithm on raw LC-MS data.
- The assay targets organisms lacking lipid standards (e.g., E. coli or archaea) where LipidCreator's eukaryote-focused coverage and the background matrix are incomplete.
- Input is untargeted/shotgun screening data rather than a LipidCreator-designed PRM or MRM transition list.
- Stereoisomer differentiation is required — no large-scale methods exist due to lack of isotope-coded standards.

## Inputs

- Background lipidome species list (e.g., yeast lipidome) with candidate lipid species
- LipidCreator-generated MS/MS fragment masses per lipid species
- Published or observed target-decoy LC/ESI PRM identification counts (true-target vs false-decoy)
- Mass tolerance settings (±0.5 Da low-resolution, ±2.5 ppm high-resolution)

## Outputs

- Fragment-count-dependent unambiguous-match probability table (0–3 fragments, both tolerances)
- Accuracy-ratio comparison against reported target-decoy accuracy (e.g., 98% at three fragments)
- validation_results.csv with probabilities, accuracy ratios, and discrepancy flags

## How to apply

Load the background lipidome species list (e.g., yeast lipidome) and enumerate all candidate lipid species and their MS/MS fragment masses generated by LipidCreator. For each fragment count (0, 1, 2, 3), compute the probability that a random precursor-fragment pair matches unambiguously within the background at ±0.5 Da tolerance, then repeat at ±2.5 ppm to quantify the effect of high-resolution filtering on a Q-Exactive HF PRM assay. Load the published target-decoy LC/ESI PRM validation results (true-target vs false-decoy identification counts) and compare the computed probabilities against the reported accuracy ratio — in the yeast case, three fragments combined across both polarity modes gave ~98% accuracy with only one decoy misidentified. Write fragment-count-dependent probabilities and accuracy comparisons into validation_results.csv, flagging any discrepancy from the published figure.

## Evaluation signals

- Computed accuracy ratio at three fragments across both polarity modes must reproduce the reported ~98% figure within the tolerance stated in the article; otherwise flag the discrepancy in the output file.
- Probability computations must be reported at both ±0.5 Da and ±2.5 ppm tolerances, showing the high-resolution filtering effect.
- Output file must contain a row per fragment count (0, 1, 2, 3) with monotonically increasing unambiguous-match probability.
- Target-decoy counts loaded must reconcile with reported results (e.g., only one decoy misidentified in the yeast assay).
- validation_results.csv must be written with both probability and accuracy-ratio columns before the skill is considered complete.

## Limitations

- LipidCreator does not provide a dedicated scoring algorithm to determine false discovery rates; it supports only knowledge-based selection of transition lists.
- LipidCreator does not address coeluting/isobaric lipids or peak area integration; retention time, elution order, and isotope-labeled standards must be considered to avoid mis-annotations.
- For unvalidated assays, multiple fragments and both polarity modes are required for unequivocal identification even in simple model systems such as yeast.
- Coverage of eukaryotic lipid classes is incomplete (some exceptions per Supplementary Data 1), and standards for bacteria such as E. coli and archaea are lacking.
- The CE prediction models are exclusive to lipid mediators; extending them requires more structurally diverse lipid standards and CE-dependent measurements.
