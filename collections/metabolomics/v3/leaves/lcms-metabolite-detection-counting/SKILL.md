# lcms-metabolite-detection-counting

> **License: noncommercial** — confirm your use is a permitted (noncommercial) purpose before applying; commercial use requires a separate license (see `metadata.tool_license`). <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Skill for quantifying how many of the in silico-predicted drug metabolites (e.g. BioTransformer 3.0 predictions) were experimentally detected by LC-HRMS after S9 fraction incubation, yielding counts and a detection-rate percentage such as 59,869 of 130,464 (45.89%).

## When to use

Use when a study has a list of predicted metabolites from a tool like BioTransformer and an LC-HRMS detection list from postincubation samples, and the goal is to compute the number (or percentage) of predictions that were actually observed.

## When NOT to use

- When the analysis goal is structural elucidation or annotation of novel (unpredicted) metabolites rather than counting predictions that were observed.
- When the input is already a summary table of detected versus predicted metabolites — this skill computes the counts and rate, not the summary itself.
- When the metabolites of interest come from extrahepatic compartments or enzymes not active in the liver S9 fraction, as these are expected to be absent from PharmMet DB.

## Inputs

- BioTransformer 3.0-predicted metabolite list for the 1114 therapeutic drugs (unique predicted metabolites count: 130,464)
- LC-HRMS detection lists from 24-hour (postincubation) human liver S9 fraction incubation samples, Study ST003991 (Metabolomics Workbench, DOI: https://doi.org/10.21228/M8W55K)

## Outputs

- summary_stats.csv with columns 'metric' and 'value', containing rows for total predicted metabolites, total detected metabolites, and detection percentage

## How to apply

Obtain the two lists: the BioTransformer 3.0-predicted metabolites for all 1114 therapeutic drugs, and the LC-HRMS detection lists from the 24-hour (postincubation) human liver S9 fraction samples deposited as Study ST003991 on Metabolomics Workbench. Cross-reference each detected postincubation mass signal to its prediction using the ±5 ppm mass-accuracy criterion applied during curation, matching on accurate mass (MS1) and, where available, retention time. Count the total unique predicted metabolites across the drug set and the number of those predictions that were detected, then compute the detection percentage (detected ÷ predicted × 100). Record both counts and the percentage as rows in a summary_stats.csv file with columns 'metric' and 'value'. This counting step relies on the upstream candidate metabolite feature detection, which compares MS1 and retention times between 0-hour and 24-hour samples.

## Related tools

- **BioTransformer 3.0** (Source of the in silico-predicted metabolite list that is cross-referenced against detected signals to count how many predictions were observed.)
- **R 4.0.3** (Used for statistical analysis and data visualization when computing the detection counts and percentage.)
- **Metabolomics Workbench ST003991** (Repository of the deposited LC-HRMS detection lists used as the source of detected metabolite signals for counting.)
- **MZmine 2.53** (Upstream raw-file processing (ADAP chromatogram builder) that produces the peak lists from which detected metabolite features are extracted.)

## Examples

```
R: write.csv(data.frame(metric=c('total_predicted','total_detected','detection_pct'), value=c(130464, 59869, round(59869/130464*100, 2))), 'summary_stats.csv', row.names=FALSE)
```

## Evaluation signals

- The computed totals should equal 130,464 predicted metabolites and 59,869 detected metabolites, matching the article's reported figures.
- The detection percentage should be 45.89% (59,869 ÷ 130,464 × 100).
- Cross-referencing must use the ±5 ppm mass-accuracy tolerance; a re-run with a different tolerance should reproduce the same counts before being accepted.
- summary_stats.csv must contain rows with metrics for predicted count, detected count, and detection rate, each with a numeric value.
- Every counted detected metabolite must trace back to a 24-hour postincubation sample signal and be matched to a unique prediction entry.

## Limitations

- The counting relies on the liver S9 fraction system; metabolites formed by enzymes not abundant or active in liver S9, or generated in extrahepatic compartments (intestine, kidney, brain), will be undercounted.
- Detection may be incomplete due to limited cofactor availability or ionization suppression during LC-HRMS.
- Novel metabolites not predicted by BioTransformer 3.0 are excluded from this count, so the metric does not capture the full metabolite diversity of each drug.
