# lc-hrms-feature-annotation-differential-presence-absence

> **License: noncommercial** — confirm your use is a permitted (noncommercial) purpose before applying; commercial use requires a separate license (see `metadata.tool_license`). <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Annotates LC-HRMS features as candidate drug metabolites by requiring their accurate MS1 m/z and retention time to be present in the 24-hour postincubation human liver S9 sample and absent in the 0-hour preincubation sample, followed by cross-referencing detected signals (±5 ppm) against BioTransformer predictions.

## When to use

Use when untargeted LC-HRMS feature tables from paired preincubation (0 h) and postincubation (24 h) human liver S9 incubation samples need candidate drug-metabolite annotation by differential presence/absence, prior to in silico curation against BioTransformer predictions.

## When NOT to use

- Input is already a curated metabolite database entry — this skill only produces candidate annotations from raw differential feature data
- The samples were not incubated with the precursor drug and cosubstrates; features detected only in samples lacking both precursor and cosubstrates fail the paper's identification criteria
- Metabolites are expected from extrahepatic compartments or enzymes not abundant or active in the liver S9 fraction — the paper states PharmMet DB is expected to lack these

## Inputs

- MZmine 2.53 feature tables with MS1 accurate m/z, retention time, and peak area per feature
- Paired 0-hour preincubation and 24-hour postincubation human liver S9 LC-HRMS sample data
- BioTransformer 3.0 predicted metabolite lists for the precursor drug

## Outputs

- Per-feature annotation table (m/z, RT, presence flags at 0 h and 24 h, candidate metabolite status)
- Curated list of observed mass signals cross-referenced with BioTransformer predictions (±5 ppm)

## How to apply

Process the raw files with MZmine 2.53 (ADAP chromatogram builder) to extract MS1 accurate m/z, retention time, and peak area for each feature in the 0-hour preincubation and 24-hour postincubation S9 samples. Compare mass spectral data (MS1) and retention times between the two time points to detect features whose signals appear only after incubation with the precursor drug plus cofactor mixture; these differential features are labelled candidate drug metabolites. Curate the detected signals by cross-referencing them with predictions from the BioTransformer data base using a ±5 ppm mass window. Write the per-feature annotation table (m/z, RT, presence flags, metabolite status) to a CSV for downstream database compilation.

## Related tools

- **MZmine 2.53** (Processes raw LC-HRMS files with ADAP chromatogram builder to produce feature tables of MS1 m/z, RT, and peak areas used for the 0-hour vs 24-hour comparison)
- **R 4.0.3** (Statistical analysis and data visualization of the differential feature detection results)
- **BioTransformer 3.0** (In silico predictor used to curate observed mass signals by cross-referencing detected features within ±5 ppm)

## Examples

```
mzmine-2.53 --raw-file raw.mzML --method ADAP --peak-list metabolite_annotation_table.csv && Rscript annotate_diff_presence.R --input metabolite_annotation_table.csv --ppm 5
```

## Evaluation signals

- Every tabulated candidate metabolite feature satisfies detection only in samples containing both precursor and cosubstrates
- Mass accuracy of matched signals is within ±5 ppm of a BioTransformer prediction
- Features show the required time-dependent increase in signal from 0 to 24 hours
- Features failing the identification criteria (presence/absence, adduct coelution, mass window) are marked as absent or non-metabolite
- 89% of tested drugs produced at least 1 metabolite not predicted on BioTransformer 3.0, so annotation lists should not be expected to be fully covered by in silico predictions

## Limitations

- Reliance upon the liver S9 fraction for biotransformation; not all drug-metabolizing activities are present in this fraction
- PharmMet DB is expected to lack metabolites formed by enzymes that are not abundant or active in the liver S9 fraction or generated in extrahepatic compartments (eg, intestine, kidney
- Limited detection of some metabolites may have occurred due to limited cofactor availability or ionization suppression
- The identification of novel metabolites from nearly 1000 drugs will require considerable research for isolation and structural elucidation
