# penalty-function-heuristic-optimization

## Summary

MetaboPAC's optimization approach infers absolute-concentration response factors from relative-abundance time-course GC-MS metabolomics data by minimizing heuristic soft-constraint penalty functions (mass-balance and biological feasibility terms), avoiding chemical standards.

## When to use

Use when relative-abundance time-course GC-MS metabolomics data lack chemical standards and you can supply a stoichiometric matrix plus some fraction of known kinetic terms, to infer response factors via soft-constraint penalty minimization.

## When NOT to use

- The sample is multi-compartment eukaryotic metabolomics with transport reactions across intracellular compartments — MetaboPAC only applies to intracellular samples in a single compartment
- Response factors vary nonlinearly with concentration (linear dynamic range or ion suppression effects) — the method assumes response factors are constant with concentration
- Absolute concentrations are already obtainable via chemical standards, making response-factor inference unnecessary

## Inputs

- Relative-abundance time-course data (concMatrix) with sampling times (timeVec) stored in generic_data.mat
- Stoichiometric matrix and known concentration/flux bounds in modelInfo_generic
- Known kinetic equations (edit calcFluxWithKinetics_generic.m / solveRF_KEapproach_generic.m)
- Custom heuristic penalty functions in the penalty_functions folder

## Outputs

- Inferred response factors per metabolite per repetition
- Per-run error-bin fractions within log2(1.1), log2(1.3), log2(1.5) tolerances
- Aggregated fractions table heuristic_noiseless_rf_error_bins.csv

## How to apply

Encode heuristic penalty functions (mass-balance feasibility, concentration/flux bounds from modelInfo) in the penalty_functions folder of MetaboPAC, then run MetaboPAC_*.m scripts on noiseless simulated data generated by the first cell of GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m over repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics values of [0 20 40 60 80 100]. For each run, compare inferred response factors against the true response factors from trueRF/ and compute the fraction falling within error-tolerance bins of log2(1.1), log2(1.3), and log2(1.5). Aggregate bin fractions per model and per known-kinetics fraction into heuristic_noiseless_rf_error_bins.csv, and statistically compare against negative-control comparator methods at each percKnownKinetics level. The rationale is that penalties reward response-factor sets consistent with metabolic mass balances, so inference improves as more kinetic terms are known a priori.

## Evaluation signals

- Inferred response-factor bin fractions significantly outperform negative-control comparator methods at each percKnownKinetics level on noiseless data across all four models
- Significant improvement over negative controls emerges when at least 20% of kinetic terms are known a priori, reproducing the Figure S5–S6 trend
- Results reproduce with repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics [0 20 40 60 80 100] following README steps 1 and 3
- Aggregated output table heuristic_noiseless_rf_error_bins.csv contains per-model, per-percKnownKinetics bin fractions covering all four metabolic models
- Penalty value of the true response-factor set is comparable to or among the lowest of candidate sets (if true RFs score markedly worse, penalty refinement is needed)

## Limitations

- Response factors were assumed constant with concentration; linear dynamic range or ion suppression may violate this assumption, and allowing nonlinear response factors would be computationally challenging
- Multiple sets of response factors had similar or even smaller penalty values than the true ones, suggesting that the penalties need to be refined
- Prediction accuracy varies widely, from 40% to nearly 100%, depending on the metabolic network, data quality, and error tolerance
- High noise levels can violate mass balances and complicate penalty-based feasibility assessment; MetaboPAC handles only intracellular, single-compartment systems without transport reactions
