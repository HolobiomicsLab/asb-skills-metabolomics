# biotransformer-reaction-type-curation

> **License: noncommercial** — confirm your use is a permitted (noncommercial) purpose before applying; commercial use requires a separate license (see `metadata.tool_license`). <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Aggregates LC-HRMS-detected human liver S9 drug metabolites by BioTransformer 3.0 biotransformation reaction type and tabulates detected-vs-predicted counts and detection fractions per reaction class (oxidation, glucuronidation, etc.). It turns the PharmMet DB curation pipeline into a quantitative reaction-type inventory.

## When to use

Use when S9-incubation LC-HRMS annotation tables carry BioTransformer 3.0 reaction-type tags and you need per-reaction detected/predicted counts (oxidation, glucuronidation, etc.).

## When NOT to use

- The metabolism of interest is extrahepatic or driven by enzymes absent or inactive in liver S9 (e.g., intestine, kidney) — S9-based reaction-type counts will not capture those metabolites.
- The input is unprocessed raw LC-HRMS data with no 0-hour vs 24-hour differential feature table yet — run MZmine 2.53 processing and candidate drug metabolite feature detection first.
- The goal is isolation and structural elucidation of novel metabolites rather than reaction-type counting of already-annotated detected features.

## Inputs

- Metabolite annotation/feature table for Study ID ST003991 (Metabolomics Workbench, DOI 10.21228/M8W55K) with per-metabolite reaction-type tags
- BioTransformer 3.0 predicted metabolite lists for the 1114 therapeutic drugs, annotated by reaction type
- MS1 feature tables from 0-hour vs 24-hour human liver S9 incubations (MZmine 2.53, ADAP chromatogram builder, dual-column HILIC+/C18− LC-HRMS on Orbitrap ID-X)

## Outputs

- Tab-separated table with columns: reaction type, detected count, predicted count, detection fraction (e.g., oxidation 30,752/59,296 = 52%)
- Reaction-type-level detected-vs-predicted comparison across phase I (oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis) and phase II (glucuronidation, methylation, acetylation, sulfation, GSH conjugation) biotransformations

## How to apply

Download the metabolite annotation tables for Metabolomics Workbench Study ID ST003991 (DOI 10.21228/M8W55K), whose candidate features were detected by comparing MS1 mass and retention times between 0-hour and 24-hour S9 incubations (raw files processed in MZmine 2.53 with ADAP chromatogram builder) and curated by cross-referencing detected signals at ±5 ppm with BioTransformer 3.0 predictions. Parse each annotation's reaction-type tag (oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis, methylation, acetylation, glucuronidation, sulfation, glutathione conjugation) and, in R 4.0.3, count experimentally detected and BioTransformer-predicted metabolites per reaction type. Write the aggregation as a tab-separated table with columns reaction type, detected count, predicted count, and detection fraction. Validate by exact match against the reported values: oxidation 30,752 detected of 59,296 predicted (52%), reduction 7114 of 10,899 (65%), hydrolysis 3184 of 5744 (55%), methylation 3136 of 6235 (50%), glucuronidation 6311 (dominant phase II), acetylation 45%, and glutathione conjugation 21%. The ±5 ppm cross-reference is the key decision point: only detected signals matched within tolerance to a predicted metabolite contribute to a reaction type's detected count.

## Related tools

- **BioTransformer 3.0** (Supplies the reaction-type-annotated predicted-metabolite reference set; detected signals are cross-referenced to it at ±5 ppm and per-reaction predicted counts derive from it)
- **MZmine 2.53** (Processes raw LC-HRMS files (ADAP chromatogram builder) into the MS1 feature tables used for the 0-h vs 24-h differential detection that feeds this curation)
- **R 4.0.3** (Aggregates detected and predicted counts per reaction type and writes the tab-separated summary table)
- **Metabolomics Workbench** (Hosts the Study ID ST003991 metabolite annotation tables that are downloaded and parsed for reaction-type tags) — https://www.metabolomicsworkbench.org

## Examples

```
Rscript -e 'write.table(counts, "reaction_type_counts.tsv", sep="\t", quote=FALSE, row.names=FALSE); stopifnot(counts["oxidation","detected"]==30752)'
```

## Evaluation signals

- Exact-match check: oxidation row must show 30,752 detected metabolites and glucuronidation 6311
- Predicted-count check: reduction 7114 of 10,899, hydrolysis 3184 of 5744, methylation 3136 of 6235 (detection fractions 65%, 55%, 50%)
- Every counted detected signal must match a BioTransformer 3.0 prediction within ±5 ppm
- Output schema check: tab-separated table with exactly the columns reaction type, detected count, predicted count, detection fraction
- Sanity range: detection fractions fall between 21% (GSH conjugation) and 65% (reduction), with phase I dominated by oxidation and phase II by glucuronidation

## Limitations

- Reliance on the liver S9 fraction means not all drug-metabolizing activities are present; metabolites formed by enzymes not abundant or active in S9, or generated in extrahepatic compartments (e.g., intestine, kidney), are expected to be missing from the reaction-type counts.
- Limited detection of some metabolites may have occurred due to limited cofactor availability or ionization suppression.
- Predicted coverage is incomplete: 89% of tested drugs produced at least 1 metabolite not predicted by BioTransformer 3.0, so no reaction-type tag is available for those novel metabolites.
