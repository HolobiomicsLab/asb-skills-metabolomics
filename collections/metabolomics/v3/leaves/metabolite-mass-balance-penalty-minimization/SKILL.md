# metabolite-mass-balance-penalty-minimization

## Summary

A MetaboPAC-based optimization approach that infers metabolite response factors (and hence absolute concentrations) from relative abundance time-course GC-MS data by penalizing solutions that violate metabolic mass balances and other biological characteristics of the system, avoiding the need for chemical standards.

## When to use

Use when time-course GC-MS metabolomics data (relative abundance concMatrix plus timeVec) are available for a single-compartment intracellular metabolic network, and absolute concentrations or response factors must be inferred without chemical standards.

## When NOT to use

- The sample is not intracellular single-compartment metabolomics — MetaboPAC assumes all metabolites are directly connected via reactions and does not consider transport reactions across intracellular compartments in eukaryotic systems
- Response factors are known to vary with concentration (nonlinear dynamic range or ion suppression), since MetaboPAC assumes response factors are constant with concentration
- The dataset has very high noise or substantial missing values that violate mass balances and complicate assessment of biological feasibility of response factors

## Inputs

- generic_data.mat with relative abundance data (concMatrix) and measurement times (timeVec)
- Stoichiometric matrix of the metabolic system plus known concentration and flux bounds (modelInfo_generic)
- Known kinetic equations (hard-coded in calcFluxWithKinetics_generic.m and solveRF_KEapproach_generic.m)
- Penalty functions for the optimization approach (penalty_functions folder)
- Noiseless or noise-added time-course data generated by GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m

## Outputs

- Inferred response factors per metabolite (aggregated by median over 20 repetitions)
- Inferred absolute metabolite concentrations from relative abundance data
- Results saved to keapproach_noiseless_results.mat (per task workflow)

## How to apply

Prepare a generic_data.mat containing relative abundance data (concMatrix) and measurement times (timeVec), then edit modelInfo_generic with the system's stoichiometric matrix and any known concentration and flux bounds, and hard-code known kinetic equations into calcFluxWithKinetics_generic.m and solveRF_KEapproach_generic.m. Define penalty functions in the penalty_functions folder that score biologically infeasible response-factor sets (mass-balance violations), then run MetaboPAC (kinetic-equation approach) over repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics values of [0 20 40 60 80 100], varying the fraction of known kinetic terms to test sensitivity. Aggregate inferred response factors and concentrations across the 20 seeded repetitions via the median to obtain per-model inferred absolute concentrations. Judge correctness by computing the fraction of inferred concentrations within log2 error tolerances: for noiseless synthetic models expect 100% within log2(1.1); for E. coli and S. cerevisiae models expect 100% within log2(1.3) with >90% within log2(1.1).

## Related tools

- **MetaboPAC** (Computational framework performing the mass-balance penalty-minimization inference of response factors and absolute concentrations; scripts MetaboPAC_*.m, MetaboPAC_*_noisy.m, MetaboPAC_*_log.m, MetaboPAC_generic.m execute it) — gtStyLab/MetaboPAC
- **MATLAB** (Runtime environment in which MetaboPAC_*.m scripts and driver_genDatasets*.m data generators are executed)

## Examples

```
matlab -batch "run('MetaboPAC_generic.m')"  % with generic_data.mat (concMatrix, timeVec) and modelInfo_generic configured
```

## Evaluation signals

- On noiseless synthetic models, 100% of inferred response factors fall within log2(1.1) error; on biological (E. coli, S. cerevisiae) models, 100% within log2(1.3) with >90% within log2(1.1)
- Median aggregation across repetitions [1:20] is applied before computing per-model inferred absolute concentrations
- Performance is checked against percKnownKinetics values of [0 20 40 60 80 100], with significant improvement over negative controls expected when 20% of kinetic terms are known a priori
- No penalty-function errors are raised and multiple response-factor sets with penalty values smaller than the true ones are inspected as a refinement signal

## Limitations

- Only applicable to intracellular metabolomics samples in a single compartment; transport reactions across eukaryotic intracellular compartments are not considered
- Response factors are assumed constant with concentration, which will not always hold; allowing nonlinear response factors would be computationally challenging and it is unclear which metabolites would need them
- Multiple sets of response factors can have similar or even smaller penalty values than the true ones, suggesting the penalties need refinement
- Accuracy of inferred concentrations is limited by the accuracy of the underlying flux calculation approaches
- High noise levels in the data can violate mass balances, and missing values with imputation reduce accuracy of inferred concentrations
