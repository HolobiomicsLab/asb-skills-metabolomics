# ppm-vs-da-tolerance-comparison

## Summary

Recomputes the false-match probability of precursor–fragment lipid identifications at coarse ±0.5 Da versus high-resolution ±2.5 ppm tolerances to quantify how mass-accuracy filtering controls target–decoy discrimination in targeted lipidomics.

## When to use

Use when evaluating how many fragments per lipid species are needed for unambiguous identification in a background lipidome (e.g. yeast) and you need to compare loose Da-level versus tight ppm-level matching tolerances.

## When NOT to use

- The background lipidome is bacterial or archaeal (e.g. E. coli acyl-PG/acyl-PE), since the article notes a lack of standards and the knowledge-base does not cover these classes.
- A dedicated FDR scoring algorithm is required — LipidCreator intentionally does not provide one and does not address coeluting/isobaric lipids or peak-area integration.
- Stereoisomer discrimination is the goal, because the article states there are currently no large-scale methods available to cope with occurring stereoisomers.

## Inputs

- Background lipidome species list (e.g. yeast lipidome candidate species)
- Enumerated precursor–fragment mass pairs per lipid species and polarity mode
- Published target–decoy LC/ESI PRM identification counts (true-target vs false-decoy)
- Tolerance settings: ±0.5 Da and ±2.5 ppm

## Outputs

- Fragment-count-dependent false-match probabilities at ±0.5 Da and ±2.5 ppm
- Accuracy-ratio comparison (computed vs reported, e.g. 98% at 3 fragments)
- validation_results.csv

## How to apply

Load the background lipidome species list (e.g. the yeast lipidome used in the LipidCreator paper) and enumerate candidate lipid species and their MS/MS fragment masses as generated by LipidCreator. For each fragment count (0, 1, 2, 3), compute the probability that a random precursor–fragment pair matches unambiguously within the background at ±0.5 Da tolerance, then repeat the identical computation at ±2.5 ppm to capture the effect of high-resolution filtering such as on the Q-Exactive HF PRM assay. In parallel, load the published target–decoy LC/ESI PRM validation counts (true-target vs false-decoy identifications) and compare the computed accuracy ratios against the reported figure (98% accuracy at three fragments across positive and negative polarity modes). Write both the fragment-count-dependent probabilities and the accuracy-ratio comparison into a validation table (e.g. validation_results.csv), flagging any mismatch beyond the article's tolerance.

## Examples

```
lipidcreator.exe --mode batch --species yeast_lipidome.csv --fragments 3 --tolerance 2.5ppm --decoy --export validation_results.csv
```

## Evaluation signals

- Computed accuracy ratio at three fragments must reproduce the reported ~98% figure across both polarity modes within the article's tolerance.
- Probabilities at ±2.5 ppm must be strictly lower than the corresponding probabilities at ±0.5 Da for every fragment count, reflecting the tighter mass filter.
- Output table must contain rows for 0, 1, 2, and 3 fragments for both tolerance regimes.
- Any deviation from the published target–decoy counts must be explicitly flagged in validation_results.csv.
- Fragment enumeration must cover the full yeast lipidome species list used as background.

## Limitations

- LipidCreator does not provide a dedicated scoring algorithm to determine false discovery rates.
- Retention time, elution order, and isotope-labeled standards always need to be considered to avoid mis-annotations.
- The majority of eukaryotic lipid classes are covered but some exceptions remain; the coverage excludes rare bacterial/archaeal lipidomes.
- There are currently no large-scale methods available to cope with occurring stereoisomers.
