# boundary-flux-calculation

> **License: noncommercial** — confirm your use is a permitted (noncommercial) purpose before applying; commercial use requires a separate license (see `metadata.tool_license`). <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Quantifies directional change of extracellular metabolite intensities between paired time points (e.g. 0 h vs 72 h Borrelia cultures) to label boundary metabolites as consumed or produced, enabling inference of missing metabolic reactions such as purine and pyrimidine pathway steps.

## When to use

Use when you have time-series extracellular metabolomics (intensities at a start and end time point) and need to infer which reactions are active or missing from a KEGG-based organism network.

## When NOT to use

- When intracellular flux estimation with isotope tracing or FBA is required for absolute reaction rates - boundary fluxes from intensity profiles are semi-quantitative only
- When the organism's metabolic network is already complete and validated, leaving no missing reactions to infer
- When the metabolomics dataset lacks paired time points or a consistent time interval, as the flux is defined by change between two time points

## Inputs

- Temporal extracellular metabolomics intensity profiles (e.g. metabolite intensities sampled at 0 h and 72 h from in-vitro cultures)
- Organism-specific metabolic network layout from MINNO (KEGG base/ortholog pathways combined with NCBI-RefSeq genetic annotation)

## Outputs

- Boundary flux assignments (produced/consumed/no-change) for each metabolite between paired time points
- Evidence for missing reactions in the organism network (e.g. inferred purine or pyrimidine reactions absent from KEGG)
- Annotated MINNO network with boundary fluxes overlaid for visual inspection

## How to apply

Culture microbes in vitro and record metabolite intensities at defined intervals (0 h and 72 h in the Borrelia application). For each metabolite, compute the direction and magnitude of intensity change between the paired time points to assign a boundary flux, treating intensities as a proxy for concentration. Overlay these boundary fluxes onto the organism-specific network generated by MINNO from KEGG base/ortholog layout and NCBI-RefSeq annotation, and look for metabolites whose flux pattern implies a reaction absent from the KEGG annotation (e.g. thymine production from thymidine implying pyrimidine-nucleoside phosphorylase). Flag metabolites with consistent, directional change as boundaries, then search for genes or proteins corresponding to any inferred missing reactions. Note that intensity-based fluxes are semi-quantitative: they indicate direction and relative magnitude, not absolute rates.

## Related tools

- **MINNO** (Loads the KEGG base/ortholog network and NCBI-RefSeq annotation, then accepts the temporal metabolite intensity profiles and boundary fluxes as overlay data to infer missing reactions) — https://github.com/LewisResearchGroup/MINNO

## Evaluation signals

- Every metabolite in the time-series profile receives a directional flux assignment between the paired time points
- Boundary fluxes map cleanly onto the MINNO network visualization without orphan metabolites
- Inferred missing reactions are supported by boundary flux direction consistent with a plausible catalytic step (e.g. thymidine → thymine indicating PnP)
- In the Borrelia application, boundary flux analysis reproduces the paper's finding that B. burgdorferi produces thymine from thymidine while B. parkeri and B. turicatae show flux patterns consistent with an unannotated ribonucleotide reductase
- Reactions inferred as missing can be independently checked against NCBI-RefSeq homology or primary literature (the paper confirmed 6 of 8 purine reactions this way)

## Limitations

- Fluxes are inferred from relative intensity changes, not absolute concentrations or calibrated rates, so they cannot distinguish transport rate changes from abundance changes without additional validation
- Boundary flux inference only reveals reactions whose metabolites show detectable intensity change between the sampled time points; silent or balanced reactions are invisible
- Missing-reaction calls from boundary fluxes remain hypotheses until confirmed by gene/protein evidence or primary literature; in the paper only 9 of 18 KEGG-missing reactions were literature-supported
- The method depends on the quality and completeness of the underlying KEGG base/ortholog layout and the organism's NCBI-RefSeq annotation
