# metabolomics-missing-value-imputation

## Summary

A nearest-neighbor imputation skill for metabolomics matrices in which missing values arise from both MAR technical dropout and MNAR censoring at the limit of detection (LOD); it centers on KNN-TN, which standardizes metabolites using truncated-normal maximum-likelihood means and SDs (LOD as the truncation point) before correlation-based KNN imputation.

## When to use

Use when a metabolite abundance matrix has missing values generated by MNAR (metabolite below the LOD) combined with MAR dropout, and imputation quality must preserve both RMSE accuracy and downstream significance-testing metabolite lists.

## When NOT to use

- Missingness is purely MAR/MCAR with no limit-of-detection truncation — the truncated-normal standardization step of KNN-TN offers no advantage, and KNN-CR, KNN-EU or mean imputation suffice.
- A metabolite's sample mean lies far above the LOD (> 3 SD away) or missingness below the LOD is excessive — Newton-Raphon truncated-normal estimation may fail to converge or yield unstable, high-variance RMSE for that metabolite.
- Metabolite distributions are strongly non-normal and cannot be log-transformed to approximate normality — the normality assumption underlying the truncated mean and variance estimation is a stated limitation.

## Inputs

- A metabolite × sample intensity matrix (metabolites in rows) with missing values, e.g. myocardial infarction (288 metabolites), atherothrombotic (1032 metabolites) or African Race data (Ethiopia 25x1251, Tanzania 15x2250)
- A known limit of detection (LOD) for each metabolite, used as the truncation point a (e.g. the 10% quantile of the complete data in simulations)
- A missingness specification separating MNAR (values below the LOD) from MAR (values removed at random), e.g. a 2:1 MNAR-to-MAR split such as 10% MNAR + 5% MAR

## Outputs

- A completed intensity matrix with missing values imputed (knn_tn_imputed.csv) with values back-transformed to the original metabolite scale
- Per-metabolite truncated-normal MLE mean and SD estimates (truncated_mle_estimates.csv), with flags for non-convergent Newton-Raphson fits
- RMSE between imputed and true values when truth is available (simulation settings), and MLCI values comparing imputed vs complete-data significant metabolite lists for downstream two-group t-tests

## How to apply

First log-transform metabolite intensities to approximately achieve normality, then apply the cleaning step that removes metabolites with more than 75% missing values. For each retained metabolite whose sample mean lies within three standard deviations of the LOD, fit a left-truncated normal with the LOD as truncation point a by maximum likelihood using Newton-Raphson (log-likelihood l = -n*ln(P(Y in (a,inf)|mu,sigma^2)) - n*ln(sqrt(2*pi*sigma^2)) - sum((y_i-mu)^2)/(2*sigma^2)), initialized at the sample mean and SD; for all other metabolites use the sample mean and SD directly. Standardize each metabolite with these truncation-based estimates, then for each missing value find the K=10 nearest metabolites by the correlation distance d_C = 1 - |r| (omitting pairwise missing observations) and impute as the sign(r_k)-weighted inverse-distance average w_k = sign(r_k)*d_k^-1 / sum_l(d_l^-1), so negatively correlated neighbors can contribute. Finally back-transform imputed values to the original scale. The choice K=10 and the three-SD eligibility rule were fixed by the source paper to accelerate runtime, since Newton-Raphson truncated-normal fitting is what makes KNN-TN (~19.95 s at 15% missingness on 50x400) slower than KNN-CR (~3.41 s) and KNN-EU (~1.81 s).

## Examples

```
Rscript knn_tn_impute.R --input metabolite_matrix.csv --lod lod.csv --K 10 --output knn_tn_imputed.csv
```

## Evaluation signals

- In simulations with known truth, KNN-TN should yield lower RMSE than KNN-CR and KNN-EU, and impute values below the LOD more accurately than Euclidean or correlation-based metrics.
- ANOVA contrasts on RMSE (KNN-TN vs KNN-CR, KNN-TN vs KNN-EU) should be statistically significant (p < 0.05) across the 20x400, 50x400 and 100x900 configurations at 9%, 15% and 30% missingness.
- Truncated-normal Newton-Raphson fits must converge (finite mu, sigma) for all metabolites whose sample mean is within 3 SD of the LOD; flag and inspect any non-convergent metabolites.
- Imputed values must be back-transformed to the original metabolite scale and remain consistent with truncation at the LOD.
- In real-data downstream evaluation, mean MLCI of KNN-TN should approach or exceed that of KNN-CR (e.g. myocardial MLCI 0.510 vs zero imputation 0.061) with significant improvement over zero, minimum and KNN-EU imputation in two of three datasets.

## Limitations

- KNN-TN relies on a normality assumption for estimating the truncated mean and standard deviation; metabolite data are often non-normal unless log-transformed.
- Estimation of the truncated normal mean and variance is unstable with excessive missing data or small samples, giving higher RMSE variability for KNN-TN than KNN-CR.
- The KNN-TN likelihood uses only observed metabolite data; the missingness mechanism (MNAR, MAR, MCAR) is unknown and is not incorporated into the likelihood.
- Newton-Raphson may fail to converge when missingness below the LOD is too high; results for sample sizes under 20 or missing percentages below those evaluated were referenced but not shown.
- Downstream analysis evaluation (MLCI on t-test metabolite lists) is preliminary; a more comprehensive study of all potential downstream analyses is still needed.
