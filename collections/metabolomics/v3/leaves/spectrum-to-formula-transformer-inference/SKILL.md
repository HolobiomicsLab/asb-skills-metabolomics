# spectrum-to-formula-transformer-inference

> **License: restricted** — no clear open-source license detected for the underlying tool; verify licensing before commercial use or redistribution. <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Score chemical formula and adduct candidates for an unknown MS/MS spectrum using MIST-CF, a chemical-formula-transformer neural network trained with energy-based modeling, replacing SIRIUS fragmentation trees with a learned subformula-assignment protocol. Enables de novo, database-independent formula annotation from tandem MS data.

## When to use

Use when you have positive-mode MS/MS spectra (mzML/MGF) with precursor masses and need de novo chemical formula (and adduct) predictions without a spectral database, e.g. benchmarking against SIRIUS/CSI:FingerID on challenge data like CASMI2022.

## When NOT to use

- Input spectra are negative-ion-mode: models were trained only for positive-mode data, so negative-mode support is absent
- You need joint scoring of MIST-CF with MS1 isotopic-pattern information — the paper does not integrate MIST-CF scores with MS1 isotopic scores
- The goal is fingerprint/structure annotation (e.g. CSI:FingerID-style) rather than chemical formula assignment

## Inputs

- Positive-mode MS/MS spectra exported from mzML via MZmine 3 (MGF format, e.g. data/demo_specs.mgf or casmi22 spectra)
- Formula candidate lists per precursor mass generated by SIRIUS decomp
- Pretrained MIST-CF checkpoint (quickstart model trained on public NPLIB1/CANOPUS data; NIST20-trained models available on request)
- Ground-truth CASMI 2022 formula/adduct assignments for scoring

## Outputs

- Per-spectrum ranked formula and adduct predictions from MIST-CF (quickstart/mist_cf_out/)
- results_table.tsv with per-spectrum predictions and the Table 2 comparison (e.g. 0.862 vs 0.516 formula top-1 accuracy vs CSI:FingerID)

## How to apply

Preprocess the LC-MS/MS mzML files with MZmine 3 feature detection/export to extract MS/MS spectra (e.g. the 304 positive-mode CASMI 2022 challenge spectra). Decompose each exact precursor mass into formula candidates using SIRIUS's fast dynamic-programming decomposition module (`SIRIUS decomp`, version 5.5.7 headless, exposed via the SIRIUS_PATH environment variable). Run the pretrained MIST-CF model — a formula transformer with sinusoidal formula embeddings, instrument-type covariates, and embedded fragment/neutral-loss subformulae — per src/mist_cf/mist_cf_score/predict_mgf.py to score each candidate's agreement with the MS/MS spectrum. Rank candidates and take the top-1 formula (and joint formula+adduct) prediction; optionally compare against out-of-the-box SIRIUS/CSI:FingerID run in parallel and score both with analysis/evaluate_pred.py.

## Evaluation signals

- On CASMI2022 positive-mode spectra, top-1 formula accuracy should reach ~0.842 (vs 0.516 for SIRIUS/CSI:FingerID) and joint formula+adduct accuracy ~0.862 (vs ~0.868 for the winning SIRIUS submission)
- Per-spectrum predictions and the comparison table are written to results_table.tsv via analysis/evaluate_pred.py
- The quickstart run on data/demo_specs.mgf (10 CASMI22 spectra) produces outputs under quickstart/mist_cf_out/ without errors
- Predicted formulas should be among the SIRIUS decomp candidate list for the observed MS1 mass, with no candidate-set leakage

## Limitations

- Models were trained only for positive-mode data; negative-mode support is absent
- MIST-CF does not integrate MS1 isotopic scores and does not consider adduct switching in MS2 subformula assignment
- The pipeline still relies on SIRIUS's algorithmic decomposition of exact masses into formula candidates due to its fast implementation
- The public quickstart model (NPLIB1/CANOPUS) may be less performant than NIST20-trained models, particularly for Orbitrap or higher-resolution data
