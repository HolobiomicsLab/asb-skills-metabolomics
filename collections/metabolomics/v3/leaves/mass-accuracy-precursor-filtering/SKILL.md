# mass-accuracy-precursor-filtering

> **License: restricted** — no clear open-source license detected for the underlying tool; verify licensing before commercial use or redistribution. <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Annotate unannotated empirical MS/MS spectra by filtering a large in silico spectral library to candidates matching the precursor mass (1 ppm and 10 ppm tolerances), then ranking candidates by cosine similarity between the empirical spectrum and SingleFrag ANN-predicted spectra.

## When to use

Use when an unannotated empirical tandem MS spectrum must be matched to a known-mass candidate set — i.e., you have precursor mass, a ~1.9M-molecule predicted spectral library, and want Top-1/Top-5/Top-10 annotation accuracy.

## When NOT to use

- You need to predict spectra for new molecules from SMILES rather than rank candidates against existing predicted spectra — use the spectra prediction workflow instead
- Empirical spectra were acquired at collision energies outside the model's low/medium/high training conditions, or lack a reliable precursor mass, making ppm filtering and cosine ranking unreliable
- The candidate molecule's peak intensities (not just presence/absence) must be predicted — SingleFrag deliberately predicts peak presence or absence per MZ position, not intensities

## Inputs

- Unannotated empirical MS/MS spectrum with known precursor mass and (for the formula-known condition) exact molecular formula
- In silico predicted spectral library from SingleFrag ANN models (ModelVal*.pickle), ~1.9M molecules
- Candidate molecule list per spectrum with precursor masses for 1 ppm / 10 ppm filtering

## Outputs

- Ranked candidate list per test spectrum ordered by cosine similarity
- Top-1 / Top-5 / Top-10 annotation accuracy table across four CE conditions and two ppm tolerances (mirroring Fig. 4a-c)

## How to apply

Load the ANN-predicted in silico spectral library (~1.9M molecules) generated by SingleFrag's per-MZ-position ANN models (ModelVal*.pickle) via the spectraPrediction.ipynb pipeline; note the released pipeline uses only ANN models because they outperformed GNN and COM. Assemble candidate lists for each test spectrum (2,401 in the published evaluation), filtering by precursor mass accuracy at 1 ppm and 10 ppm tolerances. Compute cosine similarity between each empirical spectrum and all candidate predicted spectra, separately for the four collision-energy conditions — low, medium, high, and merged spectra across all collision energies — then rank candidates by score. Evaluate annotation success as the fraction of test spectra where the true compound appears at Top-1, Top-5, or Top-10 under each condition and ppm tolerance. The merged-CE, formula-known condition is the reference operating point: Top-1 = 37.6%, Top-5 = 71.9%, Top-10 = 82.7%.

## Related tools

- **SingleFrag** (Provides the per-MZ-position ANN models (ModelVal*.pickle) that generate the predicted spectra of library candidates used for cosine-similarity ranking) — https://github.com/MaribelPR/SingleFrag
- **mol2vec** (Embeds input molecule SMILES into the 300-dimensional vector that serves as ANN input when building the predicted spectral library)

## Evaluation signals

- Under the merged (all collision energies), formula-known condition, ranking accuracy must reproduce the published Top-1 = 37.6%, Top-5 = 71.9%, Top-10 = 82.7%
- Top-k accuracies must be reported for all four CE conditions (low, medium, high, merged) and both 1 ppm and 10 ppm precursor-mass tolerances
- Ranking accuracies should match the reported ANN-model figures within expected numeric agreement when the library is regenerated via spectraPrediction.ipynb
- Each candidate set must be non-empty after 1 ppm filtering before cosine scoring; empty candidate lists indicate a precursor-mass mismatch error

## Limitations

- The published ranking metrics assume the exact molecular formula (and hence precursor mass) is known; annotation accuracy under fully unknown-formula conditions is lower and not benchmarked here
- Merged spectra across all collision energies give the best ranking, so single-CE conditions (low, medium, high) yield weaker Top-1/Top-5/Top-10 values
- SingleFrag predicts peak presence/absence per MZ position rather than intensities, so cosine similarity is computed on binary-ish predicted peak sets; the toy example in the repo contains integer-MZ spectra while the models predict at 2-decimal resolution
