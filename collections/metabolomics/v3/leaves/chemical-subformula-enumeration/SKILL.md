# chemical-subformula-enumeration

## Summary

Enumerates all chemically valid subformulae of a molecular formula (e.g. all subsets of C7H8) by recursively taking the setwise Cartesian product of per-element subformula sets, then applies heuristic bond-breaking (to depth d=3) with exhaustive hydrogen rearrangements to generate the physically plausible fragment candidates that FormulaNet and SubsetNet score in RASSP EI–MS prediction.

## When to use

Use when training or scoring RASSP models that need a candidate set of fragment subformulae and atom subsets for a small molecule (≤48 atoms, HCONFSPCl only), or when reproducing the RASSP featurization pipeline that converts molecules into graph, subformula, and spectra tensors.

## When NOT to use

- The molecule contains elements outside HCONFSPCl or exceeds 48 total atoms / 4096 unique fragment formulae — the RASSP pipeline filters these out before enumeration.
- The goal is predicting the mass spectrum of a molecule with resolution above 48 atoms for FormulaNet (or 64 for SubsetNet per the spectroscopy.ai API limits), where the enumeration is not defined.
- Exotic fragmentation schemes beyond recursive bond-breaking to depth 3 with hydrogen rearrangements are required — the authors explicitly note these were ignored.

## Inputs

- Molecular graph or formula string (e.g. C7H8) parsed from the smallmols parquet columns `inchi`/`smiles`/`rdmol` (RDKit binary blob via Chem.Mol(rdmol))
- Bond-breaking depth parameter d (default 3 in RASSP)
- Atom-count and fragment-count filters: total atoms ≤48, unique fragment formulae ≤4096, atoms restricted to HCONFSPCl

## Outputs

- Deduplicated table of enumerated chemical subformulae / fragment subformulae (e.g. toluene_fragments.csv) including the parent formula and all bond-breaking-depth fragments with hydrogen rearrangements
- Count-encoded (run-length one-hot) subformula representations consumed by FormulaNet
- Atom-subset candidate sets consumed by SubsetNet

## How to apply

Start from the molecular graph of the target molecule (e.g. toluene C7H8) and first run the recursive setwise Cartesian product enumeration `getSubformulae`, splitting the formula per element so getSubformulae(C6H12O6) = getSubformulae(C6) ⊗ getSubformulae(H12O6). Encode each subformula with the count-encoded (run-length one-hot) representation using maxelem large enough to cover all HCONFSPCl counts. For SubsetNet candidates, apply the heuristic bond-breaking scheme: recursively break every bond in the molecular graph out to depth d = 3, producing atom subsets, then perform exhaustive hydrogen rearrangements on each fragment, a well-studied transition in mass spectrometric fragmentation. Deduplicate the resulting subformula/fragment table before downstream scoring; the pipeline filters to molecules with ≤48 total atoms and ≤4096 unique fragment formulae. The RDKit-parsed `rdmol` binary blobs in the smallmols parquet (columns `mol_id`, `inchi`, `smiles`, `rdmol`, `spect`) are the expected molecular inputs, and the enumeration feeds the observation model that computes isotope-aware mass spectra per formula/subset which are probability-weighted and summed.

## Evaluation signals

- The enumerated subformula table must include the parent molecular formula (e.g. C7H8 for toluene) and fragments generated at every bond-breaking depth up to and including d = 3, each with exhaustive hydrogen rearrangements.
- The output must be a valid CSV/parquet of deduplicated subformulae whose count-encoded representation fits the run-length one-hot encoding with maxelem chosen for HCONFSPCl.
- Unique fragment formulae per molecule must not exceed 4096 and total atoms must not exceed 48; the trained demo model (e.g. checkpoint `demo.first-test.48668593.00000000.model`) loads and predicts spectra over the enumerated subformulae of held-out molecules without error.
- Forward evaluation of the trained model over the 100-molecule smallmols sample produces `forward.preds/demo.spect.sqlite` with `mol_id`, `spect`, and `phase` columns, indicating the enumeration fed a working spectrum generator.

## Limitations

- More exotic fragmentation schemes beyond recursive bond-breaking with hydrogen rearrangements are ignored and their inclusion could potentially improve recall.
- The graph neural networks used downstream consider only atoms and do not take into account bond information beyond bond order, so enumeration quality is bounded by that representation.
- Filtering excludes molecules with elements outside HCONFSPCl, more than 48 total atoms, or more than 4096 unique fragment formulae.
