# metabolic-model-response-factor-inference

## Summary

Run MetaboPAC to infer response factors and absolute metabolite concentrations from relative-abundance time-course GC-MS data by leveraging metabolic mass balances, kinetic equations, and a penalty-function optimization, including robustness testing under noise and reduced sampling frequency.

## When to use

Use when you have time-course relative-abundance GC-MS metabolomics data (concMatrix + timeVec), a stoichiometric matrix with concentration/flux bounds, and need standard-free inference of absolute concentrations or response factors.

## When NOT to use

- Metabolomics data from multiple intracellular compartments or eukaryotic systems needing transport reactions between compartments — MetaboPAC only applies to intracellular samples in a single compartment and does not model compartmental transport.
- Datasets where relative abundances cannot be tied to a metabolic network with mass balances, or where no penalty functions can be defined for the system of interest.
- Datasets with substantial missing values not addressable by imputation — 10% missingness with imputation measurably reduces accuracy of inferred concentrations.

## Inputs

- generic_data.mat with relative abundance data (concMatrix) and measurement times (timeVec)
- Stoichiometric matrix and known concentration/flux bounds (modelInfo_generic)
- Known kinetic equations (hard-coded into calcFluxWithKinetics_generic.m / solveRF_KEapproach_generic.m)
- Penalty functions in the penalty_functions folder
- In silico noiseless/noisy datasets generated by GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m

## Outputs

- Inferred response factors and absolute metabolite concentrations per model and per repetition
- Inference-quality statistics aggregated across repetitions for each (model, timepoints, CoV) combination
- Robustness tables/figures, e.g. results CSV such as results_noisy_robustness.csv

## How to apply

Assemble generic_data.mat containing relative abundance data (concMatrix) and measurement times (timeVec), edit modelInfo_generic with the system's stoichiometric matrix and any known concentration and flux bounds, and hard-code known kinetic equations into calcFluxWithKinetics_generic.m and solveRF_KEapproach_generic.m. Create penalty functions in the penalty_functions folder, then run MetaboPAC_generic_noisy.m (or the system-specific MetaboPAC_*.m drivers) over repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics values of [0 20 40 60 80 100]. For noisy-data robustness, generate noise-added datasets with timepoints [50 15], coefficient-of-variation values [5 15] (%), and repetitions [1:20] using the second cell of GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m, applying impulse-function smoothing to noisy time courses. Aggregate inference-quality statistics across the 20 repetitions per (model, timepoints, CoV) combination; expect the determined synthetic model to be robust to reduced sampling rate but noise-sensitive, and the underdetermined-with-regulation model to be noise-robust but sampling-sensitive.

## Evaluation signals

- MetaboPAC performs significantly better than negative control approaches when 20% of kinetic terms are known a priori.
- Under lower sampling frequency (15 timepoints) and high noise (15% CoV), significant inference of concentrations is achieved in 3 of the 4 studied models.
- The determined synthetic model is robust to decreased sampling rate but sensitive to added noise; the underdetermined-with-regulation model is more robust to high noise than to reduced sampling frequency.
- Results reproduce the manuscript figures when driven by the documented sequence: noiseless generation, noisy generation (timepoints [50 15], CoV [5 15]), and MetaboPAC_*.m runs over repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics [0 20 40 60 80 100].
- Predicted accuracy falls in the reported 40%–near-100% range depending on network, data quality, and error tolerance; deviations should be recorded in the results CSV.

## Limitations

- MetaboPAC assumes all metabolites are directly connected via reactions and only applies to intracellular metabolomics samples in a single compartment; it does not consider transport reactions across intracellular compartments in eukaryotic systems.
- Response factors were assumed constant with concentration; linear dynamic range limits or ion suppression may violate this assumption, and allowing nonlinear response factors would be computationally challenging.
- Accuracy of flux calculation depends on existing/in-development approaches, limiting MetaboPAC inference accuracy.
- High noise levels can violate mass balances and complicate assessment of biological feasibility; multiple response-factor sets can attain similar or smaller penalty values than the true ones, suggesting penalties need refinement.
- Prediction accuracy varies widely (from 40% to nearly 100%) across metabolic networks, data quality, and error tolerances.
