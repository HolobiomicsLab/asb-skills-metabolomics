# ms2-precursor-coisolation-analysis

## Summary

Quantify co-fragmentation (chimeric MS2 spectra) in a simulated or measured LC-MS/MS run by annotating each MS2 spectrum's peaks against theoretical fragment ions from all co-eluting precursor peptides, yielding per-spectrum peptide multiplicity statistics.

## When to use

Use when you have a simulated (e.g., SMITER-generated mzML) or real complex-mixture dataset and must estimate how many distinct peptides co-fragment within each MS2 spectrum before committing to a real MS experiment.

## When NOT to use

- The input is a single-peptide or well-separated dataset with no overlapping retention times, where chimericity cannot arise and the analysis adds no information.
- The goal is de novo peptide identification from real spectra rather than quantifying co-fragmentation prevalence in a ground-truth (gold-standard) simulation.
- The data are not LC-MS/MS tandem spectra in a parseable format such as mzML with known precursor candidates.

## Inputs

- Simulated or measured complex-mixture mzML file (MS2 spectra with retention times)
- List/table of candidate precursor peptides with chemical formulas and retention times (e.g., SMITER peak properties CSV)
- Theoretical fragment ions per candidate peptide from smiter.fragmentation_functions.PeptideFragmentor

## Outputs

- chimeric_spectra_statistics.csv with per-scan peptide multiplicity, chimeric-spectrum counts (two co-fragmented molecules), counts of spectra with ≥3 peptide contributions, and max peptides-per-spectrum

## How to apply

Load the complex-mixture mzML dataset (e.g., 74,067 peptides with overlapping simulated and measured retention times) and parse its MS2 spectra. Annotate each MS2 spectrum's peaks against theoretical fragment ions generated by the SMITER PeptideFragmentor for candidate precursor peptides. Per scan, determine which peptides co-elute at the scan retention time and contribute fragments, giving the number of distinct peptides per spectrum. Tally spectra with exactly two molecules fragmented simultaneously, spectra containing fragments of three or more peptides, and the maximum peptides-per-spectrum multiplicity; write results to chimeric_spectra_statistics.csv. In the source study this yielded 13,830 chimeric spectra, 2,032 spectra with fragments of ≥3 peptides, and a maximum of nine peptides per spectrum (occurring twice), against which tallies can be verified.

## Related tools

- **SMITER** (Simulates the LC-MS/MS run and provides the PeptideFragmentor used to generate the theoretical fragment ions against which MS2 peaks are annotated) — https://github.com/LeidelLab/SMITER
- **Python** (Scripting environment used to parse mzML spectra, perform co-elution/fragment annotation, and tally chimeric-spectrum statistics) — https://pypi.python.org/pypi/smiter

## Examples

```
from smiter.fragmentation_functions import PeptideFragmentor; frags = PeptideFragmentor().fragment(peptide)
```

## Evaluation signals

- Total chimeric spectra (two molecules selected for fragmentation simultaneously) reproduce the expected scale of the study (13,830 on the 74,067-peptide dataset).
- Count of spectra with fragments of three or more peptides matches (2,032 in the reference run).
- Maximum peptides-per-spectrum multiplicity is bounded and matches the reported max (nine peptides per spectrum, occurring twice).
- Every annotated spectrum's contributing peptides co-elute at the scan retention time (co-elution check per scan).
- Statistics are written to chimeric_spectra_statistics.csv with per-scan tallies that sum to the parsed number of MS2 scans.

## Limitations

- Results depend on the fidelity of the simulated fragmentation and noise models; SMITER offers several peptide fragmentation methods and its modular design means model choice affects chimericity counts.
- The analysis presupposes known ground truth (which peptides were simulated and their retention times), so it is only directly applicable to gold-standard/simulated datasets.
- SMITER calculations are based on chemical formulas, so molecules without defined formulas or fragmentation models (only peptides, two nucleoside models, and one lipid model ship by default) cannot be assessed.
