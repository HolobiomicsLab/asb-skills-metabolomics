# simsiam-contrastive-loss-implementation

## Summary

Implement the SimSiam-style stop-gradient contrastive loss used in DeepION to train ResNet18-based twin encoders that produce 512-dimensional ion image representations without manual annotation.

## When to use

Use when training a self-supervised encoder on ion images from mass spectrometry imaging, where two augmented views of each ion image must be mapped to representation vectors and collapse must be prevented via a stop-gradient contrastive loss.

## When NOT to use

- When the downstream task needs only the final 20-dimensional dense vectors for similarity measurement — the dimensionality reduction module (UMAP), not the SimSiam loss itself, is the right stage.
- When augmentations for the two views come from different ion images rather than two augmented views of a same image; the contrastive loss assumes a positive pair from one image.
- When the goal is supervised ion classification with manual annotations — DeepION is explicitly designed to generate representations self-supervised without manual annotation.

## Inputs

- Two augmented views of each ion image (generated by DeepION's modality-specific augmentation module, e.g. color jitter, filtering, Poisson noise, and missing-value augmentation)
- Preprocessed MSI matrix data with two-dimensional shape [X*Y, P] (e.g. DATASET/Pos_brain_data_matrix.txt)
- Preprocessed MSI peak data with one-dimensional shape [P, 1] (e.g. DATASET/Pos_brain_data_peak.csv)

## Outputs

- 512-dimensional representation vector (r1, r2) per ion image view from the twin ResNet18-based encoders
- Trained encoder and prediction module weights optimized by the SimSiam stop-gradient loss
- Contrastive loss value per training step reflecting the asymmetric stop-gradient terms

## How to apply

Define a parameter-sharing twin encoder f(•|θf) whose backbone is a pretrained ResNet18 and that outputs 512-dimensional representation vectors r1 and r2 from two augmented views of the same ion image. Feed each representation through a three-layer MLP projection head g(•|θg) yielding h1 and h2, then through a two-layer MLP prediction head yielding p1 and p2; the prediction head is what keeps the model from producing collapse results. Compute the SimSiam contrastive loss with the stop-gradient operation so the encoder on x2 receives no gradient from h2 in the first loss term but receives gradients in the second term, and vice versa for x1. Initialize all parameters from N(0, 0.02) and train only the encoder and prediction module with Adam (learning rate 0.0003, beta1 0.5, beta2 0.99). Smoke-test the assembled modules on a preprocessed MSI matrix such as DATASET/Pos_brain_data_matrix.txt to confirm each view produces a 512-dimensional representation before scaling to the full rat brain tissue dataset.

## Evaluation signals

- The encoder output dimension equals 512 (d = 512) for each augmented view fed through the twin networks.
- The loss executes the asymmetric stop-gradient: the encoder on x2 receives no gradient from h2 in the first loss term but receives gradients in the second term, and vice versa for x1.
- The prediction head (two-layer MLP) is present between the projection and the loss, preventing collapse results during training.
- All model parameters are initialized from N(0, 0.02) and the Adam optimizer is configured with learning rate 0.0003, beta1 0.5, beta2 0.99.
- A forward pass on DATASET/Pos_brain_data_matrix.txt runs end-to-end and produces non-collapsed (non-constant) representation vectors.

## Limitations

- DeepION was validated on rat brain tissue MSI; transferability to other tissue types or acquisition platforms is not demonstrated in the cited evidence.
- The reduced representation dimension is fixed at 20 downstream, chosen by a trade-off between space–time complexity and information utilization, which may discard spectral detail.
- Requires modality-specific augmentations (COL: color jitter, filtering, Poisson noise, random missing value; ISO adds intensity-dependent missing value) matched to the Poisson distribution and missing patterns of MSI data — naive generic augmentations may degrade the contrastive training.
- Training details such as batch size, number of epochs, and learning-rate schedules beyond the stated Adam parameters are not reported in the article.
