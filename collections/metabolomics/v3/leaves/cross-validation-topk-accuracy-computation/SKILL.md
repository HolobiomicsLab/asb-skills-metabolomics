# cross-validation-topk-accuracy-computation

## Summary

Benchmarking procedure that evaluates whether a generative chemical language model (DeepMet) prioritizes the correct chemical structure of a held-out metabolite using only exact mass, by ranking mass-filtered candidate structures by sampling frequency and computing top-1/top-3/top-10 accuracies against an AddCarbon baseline.

## When to use

Use when evaluating a structure generator's ability to recover held-out metabolites from exact monoisotopic mass alone, or when comparing sampling-frequency-based structure prioritization against a baseline like AddCarbon.

## When NOT to use

- When MS/MS spectra or retention times are available and a spectral-matching annotation approach should be used instead — this benchmark uses exact mass only
- When the target organism's metabolome is evolutionarily distant from human (e.g. plant or bacterial metabolism), since the model was trained and evaluated only on human metabolite structures and likely requires a bespoke model

## Inputs

- Held-out metabolite list with SMILES/InChIKey (cross-validation test folds and prospective HMDB v5.0 set)
- Sampled-molecule files from clm Snakemake collect_tabulated_molecules / process_tabulated_molecules steps (molecular mass, formula, sampling frequency per structure)
- AddCarbon-generated baseline structure database

## Outputs

- evaluation_results.csv with top-1/top-3/top-10 accuracies, rank distributions, AddCarbon-vs-DeepMet comparisons, and calibration-bin statistics

## How to apply

Load held-out metabolite lists (cross-validation test folds and the prospective HMDB v5.0 set) together with sampled-molecule files produced by the clm Snakemake pipeline's collect_tabulated_molecules / process_tabulated_molecules steps, which tabulate molecular mass, formula, and sampling frequency per structure. For each held-out metabolite, compute its exact monoisotopic mass with RDKit and filter sampled structures to those within ±10 ppm of the target. Sort retained candidates in decreasing sampling frequency and record the rank at which the correct structure appears (matched by SMILES/InChIKey), mirroring the write_structural_prior_CV logic; repeat for the AddCarbon baseline database generated by inserting a random carbon atom into training molecules. Aggregate ranks across held-out metabolites into top-1/top-3/top-10 accuracies, and bin confidence (sampling-frequency-derived) scores for correct vs incorrect annotations to check calibration of the prior. Write accuracies, rank distributions, AddCarbon-vs-DeepMet comparisons, and calibration-bin statistics to evaluation_results.csv.

## Evaluation signals

- Cross-validation top-1/top-3/top-10 accuracies reproduce approximately 29%/51%/72%
- Prospective (HMDB v5.0) top-1/top-10 accuracies reproduce approximately 22.9%/61.7%
- DeepMet accuracy exceeds the AddCarbon baseline on the same held-out metabolites
- Confidence-bin statistics show the sampling-frequency-derived prior is well-calibrated (correct annotations concentrate in higher-frequency bins)
- Serotonin example: the correct structure appears among high-frequency mass-filtered candidates

## Limitations

- The benchmark uses only exact mass as input; accuracy in absence of other analytical data, though 'remarkably accurate', is still partial (top-1 ≈ 29% in cross-validation)
- Model was trained and evaluated on human metabolite structures; plant or bacterial metabolism likely requires a bespoke model
- The language model has inherent limitations anticipating metabolites from divergent, as-of-yet undiscovered biosynthetic routes
