# concentration-inference-accuracy-benchmarking

## Summary

Benchmark the fraction of absolute metabolite concentrations inferred from relative-abundance time-course GC-MS data that fall within a log2(1.3) error tolerance, by running MetaboPAC's kinetic-equation-plus-heuristic-optimization workflow over known-kinetic-term percentages and comparing per-model accuracy against reported reference values.

## When to use

Use when evaluating how accurate an absolute-concentration inference pipeline is on simulated or real time-course metabolomics data — i.e. when you need per-model accuracy metrics at defined kinetic-knowledge levels rather than just qualitative 'it works' claims.

## When NOT to use

- Input data are real uncalibrated LC-MS or GC-MS measurements with no known stoichiometric matrix or kinetic equations — MetaboPAC's benchmarking assumes a defined metabolic model and known kinetic terms to set up the percKnownKinetics sweep.
- The metabolomics sample is not intracellular in a single compartment — the article states MetaboPAC 'can only be used for intracellular metabolomics samples in a single compartment' and does not model transport reactions across intracellular compartments.
- Data contain substantial missing values (e.g. 10% missingness imputed) — the article notes missing-value imputation makes inferred concentrations less accurate, so benchmarking against noiseless reference numbers would be misleading without appropriate caveats.

## Inputs

- Noiseless relative-abundance time-course data (concMatrix with timeVec) generated by the first cell of GenData/driver_genDatasets*.m
- Noise-added data at sampling frequencies of 50 or 15 timepoints and CoV of 5 or 15 (second cell of driver_genDatasets*.m)
- True response factors sampled from uniform (1–1000) or log-uniform (1–1000) distributions, 20 replicate sets per system
- Stoichiometric matrix and concentration/flux bounds per metabolic model (modelInfo files)
- Known kinetic equations for a subset of metabolites (percKnownKinetics ∈ [0 20 40 60 80 100])

## Outputs

- Per-model accuracy metrics (fraction of response factors / inferred concentrations within log2(1.3) of truth) at each percKnownKinetics level
- Separate accuracy figures for determined and underdetermined metabolites
- Aggregated benchmark table (e.g. metabopac_noiseless_metrics.csv)
- Figures from the plots folder comparing accuracy across models and kinetic-knowledge levels

## How to apply

Run the MetaboPAC_*.m scripts in MATLAB for repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics values of [0 20 40 60 80 100], letting the kinetic-equation approach first fix response factors of metabolites with known kinetics before heuristic optimization of the remaining response factors. Compute accuracy as the fraction of inferred absolute concentrations matching true concentrations within log2(1.3) error, separating determined and underdetermined metabolites as the article does. Compare resulting metrics to the paper's reference benchmarks: determined metabolites at 20% known kinetics ≈ 50%, underdetermined metabolites at 20% ≈ 83%, synthetic model at 80% > 90%, E. coli at 80% ≈ 77%, and S. cerevisiae at 80% ≈ 52%. For noisy-data variants, run MetaboPAC_*_noisy.m with timepoints [50 15], CoV [5 15], and for log-uniform response-factor variants use MetaboPAC_*_log.m with percKnownKinetics values of 100. Aggregate per-model, per-percKnownKinetics accuracy into a table (e.g. metabopac_noiseless_metrics.csv) for direct comparison against reported numbers.

## Examples

```
MATLAB: run(`"Matlab" -batch "MetaboPAC_ecoli"`) for repetitions [1:20] and percKnownKinetics [0 20 40 60 80 100], then compare accuracy vs log2(1.3) benchmarks.
```

## Evaluation signals

- At 20% known kinetic terms, determined metabolites should reach ≈50% accuracy within log2(1.3) error and underdetermined metabolites ≈83%.
- At 80% known kinetic terms, accuracy should exceed 90% for the synthetic model, ≈77% for the E. coli model, and ≈52% for the S. cerevisiae model.
- MetaboPAC should outperform negative control approaches when 20% of kinetic terms are known a priori.
- Under low sampling frequency (15 timepoints) and high noise (CoV 15), MetaboPAC should still provide significant inference of concentrations in 3 of 4 models studied.
- Each repetition uses a distinct true response-factor set (20 replicate sets per system), so accuracy values should be reported as the fraction across [1:20] runs.

## Limitations

- MetaboPAC only applies to intracellular metabolomics samples in a single compartment and does not take transport reactions across intracellular compartments in eukaryotic systems into consideration.
- Response factors were assumed constant with concentration, but this assumption will not always hold (linear dynamic range or ion suppression may violate it).
- Prediction accuracy varies widely (40% to nearly 100%) depending on network, data quality, and error tolerance.
- High noise levels can lead to violations of mass balances and complicate assessment of biological feasibility of response factors.
- Flux calculation accuracy depends on existing/in-development approaches and limits MetaboPAC inference accuracy.
